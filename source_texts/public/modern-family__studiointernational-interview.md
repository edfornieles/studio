# Modern Family — Ed Fornieles interview: 'If you want access to the site, it's a trade of your photos or data' (Studio International)
_source: https://www.studiointernational.com/index.php/ed-fornieles-modern-family-television-chisenhale-gallery · accessed 2026-06-10_

**Author:** Harriet Thorpe
**Date:** Published 16 October 2014
**Exhibition:** Modern Family, Chisenhale Gallery, London, 19 September – 9 November 2014

## Installation overview

Fornieles created an immersive Los Angeles suburban backyard environment featuring bright film-set lighting with flickering effects. The chaotic scene included a fruit pie adhered to a trellis, an oversized banana on a picnic table, and scattered muddied children's toys. Decay and excess characterised the space, with uneaten food piling up and a wire sculpture disrupting a hot tub. The lighting cycled rhythmically, with music alternating between loud and quiet.

## Connection to the TV show

The exhibition references the American sitcom *Modern Family*, which examines contemporary family structures. Fornieles noted the show presents "a genuine mutation of what family is," incorporating diverse family configurations including same-sex partnerships, which offers "a liberal version of the American family."

## Online component & data aggregation

The accompanying website required Facebook login for access. Fornieles explained: "If you want access to the site, it's a trade of your photos or data." The platform aggregated thousands of images from Tumblr, Instagram, Pinterest and Google Images using hashtags, displaying them across gallery screens. He stated: "I'm interested in the idea of embodying a platform whereby I'm trading in the commodity that is data."

## Key thematic elements

Large wire sculptures displayed text reading "Be Yourself" and "We are one," exploring tensions between individual and collective identity. Fornieles discussed nostalgia for television's passive consumption: "We lost something when we lost TV, which was the ability to not click."
