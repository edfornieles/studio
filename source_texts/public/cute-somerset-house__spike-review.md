# CUTE — Spike Art Magazine review (Fornieles film detail)

_source: https://spikeartmagazine.com/articles/review-cute-somerset-house-london-2024 · accessed 2026-06-10_

**By Habib William Kherbek — 3 April 2024**

Key passage on Ed Fornieles (verbatim):

"Ed Fornieles's (*1983) _Fini Season One: Adventures in Symbiotic Love Tyranny_ (2023) features an almost criminally chère chihuahua with a human voice, and an agenda that recapitulates the charismatic megafauna narrative (in this case, a mini-fauna): If we activate the cuteness center of your brain, you'll make room for us in your anthropic tyranny."

Concluding line of the review (verbatim): "Is it a hopeful message? Cuteness as the criterion of survival? It depends on how cute you think Roko's Basilisk is."

Opening line (verbatim): "Layering hyperpop, globalized kawaii, and charismatic megafauna over loads of Hello Kitty, an exhibition in London cashes in on our tyrannical feelings towards cuteness."

Wider review (closely transcribed): The show asks whether cuteness is "a floppy lil sword that cuts through everything," or a shield obscuring uncomfortable truths. Hello Kitty (50th anniversary) embodies a contradiction — comfort that simultaneously demands consumption; the character "emerged during a Japanese recession." The display traces kawaii's globalisation and manga's "doe-eyed" aesthetic (e.g. Junichi Nakahara) and the dynamics of desire/spectatorship. Contemporary artists on the second floor express ambivalence: Bart Seng Wen Long's videos show "impossible cuteness" rendering human existence obsolete; Cosima von Bonin's _KILLER WHALE WITH LONG EYELASHES I_ presents an exhausted beached creature, invoking "charismatic megafauna." "Works by Isaac Lythgoe and Ed Fornieles present futuristic, non-human cute entities whose survival depends on activating human emotional responses, raising unsettling questions about affection's evolutionary logic."
