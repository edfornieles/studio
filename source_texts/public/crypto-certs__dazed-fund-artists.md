# Crypto Certs — Ed Fornieles's new project proposes a new way to help fund artists (Dazed)
_source: https://www.dazeddigital.com/art-photography/article/42884/1/ed-fornieles-crypto-certs-bitcoin-project-proposes-way-to-finance-artists · accessed 2026-06-10_

**Publication:** Dazed
**Author:** Dan Heyes
**Date:** 17 January 2019

Ed Fornieles established himself as a prominent post-internet artist through immersive London performances, including a gallery frat party at Guest Projects ("Animal House", 2011) and an awards ceremony honoring Zac Efron at Serpentine Gallery Pavilion ("The Dreamy Awards", 2012). His multimedia practice explores cultural positioning, social anxiety, and peer pressure along the boundary between reality and digital spaces.

For his latest initiative, Fornieles addresses economic constraints affecting contemporary artists and galleries by proposing Crypto Certs, a blockchain-based financing model designed to decentralize art funding and distribute investment risk across broader networks of supporters rather than concentrated collector bases.

## How Crypto Certs Works

The certificates are original artworks priced at €550. Purchasers acquire both the physical print and a financial stake in the artist's future earnings. The system employs Ethereum blockchain technology, with each certificate containing an embedded key beneath a scratch panel. Holders gain access to an expanding fund generated through certificate sales. When collectors decide to liquidate their investment, scratching the panel reveals the key, destroying the artwork while releasing their accumulated profits.

Fornieles explains the mechanism: "It's another way of sourcing capital for production and then rewarding that upfront cash with a percentage of the profits."

## Artistic Philosophy

Fornieles acknowledges the model's complexity: "The current system rewards repetition of past successes, so you end up getting into loops of repeating the same type of work over and over again just for the sake of survival."

He characterizes Crypto Certs as conceptually troubling, pointing toward futures "where everyone is trading themselves on an open market, selling their futures in the hope they might transcend and reach blue-chip status."

The project was inspired by Brad Troemel's artwork embedding ten Bitcoins in physical form, sold for £5,000 before cryptocurrency's value surge. Fornieles contemplates what happens when artistic integrity becomes secondary to financial speculation: collectors may destroy artworks to access profits, raising fundamental questions about art's essential nature.
