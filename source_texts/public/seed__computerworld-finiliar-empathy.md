# Seed — Computerworld: "Finiliar by Ed Fornieles turns data into an empathy tool" (Thomas Macaulay)
_source: https://www.computerworld.com/article/1627715/ · accessed 2026-06-10_

Author: Thomas Macaulay. Publication: Computerworld UK. Date: 12 July 2019. Category: Feature (Enterprise Applications / Analytics).

Summary (paraphrase, with quotes): British artist Ed Fornieles created the Finiliar, a digital creature whose emotional state responds to real-time stock market data. The project functions as what Fornieles calls an "empathy tool," designed to forge emotional connections to abstract financial systems.

The artwork's mechanism: rising share prices cause the anime-inspired figure to smile, while declining stocks produce sadness and illness. One version links directly to Facebook stock performance.

Fornieles: "This one is attached to the stocks of Facebook. And it's very happy at the moment."

He conceived an accompanying animated film titled *Tulip Fever*, referencing the 17th-century Dutch market collapse (tulip mania).

The artist intended to demonstrate how individuals possess "miniscule" agency within larger systemic forces. He worked with Montreal-based designers to determine that human facial features most effectively generate empathy toward abstract concepts.

On broader applications, Fornieles: "This could also be attached to carbon emissions. It could be attached to the rainforest."
