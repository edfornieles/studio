# CUTE — Finiliar / Fini background (the Fornieles franchise behind the film)

_sources: https://opensea.io/blog/articles/finiliar ; https://www.carlosishikawa.com/artists/edfornieles/ ; https://www.somersethouse.org.uk/a-z-community-directory/ed-fornieles · accessed 2026-06-10_

The CUTE film _Fini Season One: Adventures in Symbiotic Love Tyranny_ extends Fornieles' Finiliar / Fini project.

**Finiliar / Fini (background):** Finiliar are "cute, tiny creatures" tethered to live financial data; their moods, movements and inflections fluctuate with global markets, currencies, stocks and (for the NFTs) cryptocurrencies — adjusting at intervals such as once an hour, twice a day, or once a week. The name has been shortened over time from "Finiliar" to "Fini." The original Fini was a 2016 GIF tethered to an RSS feed of currencies; Fornieles exhibited it through multimedia installations from New York to Tokyo from 2016. In January 2022 Fornieles partnered with curator Sam Spike to bring an NFT version — "the NFT version of a Tamagotchi" — to life. The work has been described as "animal software" that makes abstract financial data visible and relatable through cute digital creatures.

Fornieles "researched what elicits empathy within a viewer, tapping into the kawaii aesthetic, which comes from a biological connection to babies and a biological imperative to care." Fornieles (per OpenSea): "They were these cute embodiments of data that existed, using RSS feeds and large screens, sculptures and some film work."

**Artist (Carlos/Ishikawa):** Ed Fornieles, b. 1983 UK, lives/works London. MA Sculpture, Royal College of Art (2011); BA Fine Art, Ruskin School, Oxford (2005). Selected: _Inside Out 2_ (2024), _Associations_ (2021), _Cel_ (2019), _Seed_ (2017), _The Hangover Part II_ (2011); solo _The Finiliar_ at Arsenal, New York (2017). _Cute_ (Somerset House, 2024) listed in exhibition history.

**Practice (Somerset House profile):** "Ed examines the invisible structures that shape identity — the habits, protocols, and narratives we inhabit — and how they often feel immutable. Through immersive simulations, role-play, installation, sculpture, digital media, and performance, his work constructs alternative social realities to disrupt and remap those frameworks."
