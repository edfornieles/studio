# Do Disturb (Palais de Tokyo) — event page + press release
_source: https://palaisdetokyo.com/en/evenement/do-disturb-festival-non-stop-performance-danse-cirque-design/ · accessed 2026-06-10_
_corroborating source: https://www.claudinecolin.com/en/1312-do-disturb (Claudine Colin Communication press release) · accessed 2026-06-10_

**Festival title:** DO DISTURB — Festival non-stop : performance, danse, cirque, design…
**Edition:** 2nd edition
**Dates:** 8–10 April 2016
**Venue:** Palais de Tokyo, 13 avenue du Président Wilson, 75116 Paris

**Description (Palais de Tokyo, verbatim excerpt):**

"more than 50 experimental projects, where circus arts, performance arts, dance, design, fashion and sound will intermingle." The festival presented new creations and European performance debuts, in collaboration with French and international art colleges, circus schools, and design institutions.

**Programming / pricing:** Friday–Sunday festival access (€15 full / €12 reduced), with a special nighttime event on the night of 8 April (11pm–6am) featuring DJ sets and special guests.

**Participating artists (combined from both sources):**
Marwa Arsanios, Rachel Armstrong, Mircea Cantor, Arnaud Cohen, Jean-Charles de Quillacq, **Ed Fornieles**, Gerard & Kelly (*Reusable Parts / Endless Love*), Lola Gonzàlez, Trajal Harrell, Rolf Hughes, La cellule, Charlie Le Mindu, Ligia Lewis, Mel O'Callaghan, Ollie Palmer, Johannes Paul Raether, Luca Resta, Olle Sandberg, Marga Weimans (opening parade); plus students from Central Saint Martins, Design Academy Eindhoven, Stockholm University of the Arts, ENSBA Lyon, and ESAAA Annecy.

**Reference to Ed Fornieles (Claudine Colin press release, verbatim):**

"Ed Fornieles, whose work explores the formatted subjectivity of a generation bottle-fed with web 2.0 will also be exhibiting."

**Important:** Neither the Palais de Tokyo event page nor the Claudine Colin press release gives a **title** for Fornieles' contribution. The title "The Fortress of Solitude" is NOT attached to Do Disturb in either Palais de Tokyo source. (See the Somerset House source file for where that title actually appears.)
