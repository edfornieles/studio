# Der Geist: Flesh Feast — Frieze review (Thomas Bettridge, 2016)

_source: https://www.frieze.com/article/ed-fornieles-0 · accessed 2026-06-10 (text via search snippets; page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch)_

Review of "Ed Fornieles at Arratia Beer" (Berlin), by Thomas Bettridge, Frieze, 2016. Described as Fornieles' first solo exhibition in Berlin, chronicling the artist's experience following a corporate-zen style "self-management diet," documented through a series of sculptures, cartoonish infographics, banners, and a film.

**Components described:**
- Mounted on the walls are vacuum-sealed packages containing espresso beans and fabric. These allude to the starter-packs that Fornieles was selling at the exhibition, the espresso beans being the basis for Bulletproof Coffee — a mixture of espresso and butter that is the staple of the diet.
- A series of glass vitrines holds 3D-printed figurines of a cartoon fox, a recurring character in Fornieles's work. They depict various scenes of self-improvement: the fox lying on a psychoanalyst's couch; the fox doing posture exercises on a chair; the fox sitting at his desk with eight Shiva-like arms that hold various corporate utensils.
- A sculpture features two creatures made of vegetables and lemons.
- The exhibition bears a relation to the Bulletproof Diet, a high-fat, low-carb diet trend invented by Dave Asprey.

**Critical line:** The review argues the exhibition "regurgitates diet rhetoric and self-help slogans without bothering to posit anything beyond that very same white-collar value system," noting something inherently corporate about how the body is self-administered in these lifestyle regimes.
