## Early Life and Education

Edward Fornieles was born on April 6, 1983, in Petersfield, Hampshire, England. His parents operated an architectural practice in the area. He attended the University of Oxford, earning a Bachelor of Fine Arts from the Ruskin School of Drawing and Fine Art in 2005. He subsequently studied at the Royal College of Art in London, completing a Master of Arts in sculpture in 2011. During his graduate studies, he served as a studio assistant to sculptor Anish Kapoor, where he developed technical proficiency.

## Early Career and Gallery Establishment

Based in East London, Fornieles worked as a freelance data analyst while pursuing artistic and curatorial endeavors. In 2007, he co-founded the Wallis Gallery with Ross McNicol and Vanessa Carlos. During this period, his artistic approach involved "mocking, often with a dark sense of humour, the high seriousness of the London art scene."

## Performance Art and Major Works

Fornieles gained recognition through performance pieces beginning in 2011. *Animal House* (2011) at Guest Projects in London established his methodology of engaging participants in deliberately uncomfortable situations. The work involved "engaging participants and viewers in sometimes awkward situations."

*Dorm Daze* emerged from his investigation of American fraternity culture, executed as a large-scale Facebook performance where participants assumed profiles of actual American college students. *The Dreamy Awards* (2012) presented at Serpentine Galleries Park Nights featured over 200 participants, including actor Zac Efron in a pre-recorded segment, examining award ceremony culture.

*New York New York Happy Happy* (2013) involved organizing a gala at the New Museum for Rhizome. The event generated controversy because Fornieles scripted both actual museum donors and professional actors, deliberately obscuring reality. *Modern Family* (2014) was described as "a sullen installation" exploring family breakdown both metaphorically and literally.

## Gallery Exhibitions and Installations

His first solo gallery exhibition, *The Hangover Part II* (2011), was constructed from materials leftover from *Animal House* at Carlos/Ishikawa Gallery, London. *Characterdate, Frame* (2012) at Frieze Art Fair London continued exploring American college culture through sculptural installation.

*Despicable Me 2* (2013) at Mihai Nicodim Gallery in Los Angeles created a female character, "Britney Rivers," demonstrating personality development through social media networking. *Jupiter Ascending* (2015) at Carl Kostyal in Stockholm recreated a meditative spa environment reflecting body-care culture. *Der Geist: Flesh Feast* (2016) at Arratia Beer in Berlin combined film, objects, and installation exploring self-management dieting practices.

## Group Exhibitions

He participated in significant Post-Internet group shows including *Meanwhile... Suddenly, and Then* (curated by Gunnar B. Kvaran) for the 12th Biennale d'art contemporain de Lyon; *Family state of mind* (2014) with Petra Cortright at Galerie Chez Valentin in Paris; and *I feel we think bad* (2016) with Hannah Perry and Matt Goerzen at Arsenal in Montreal.

## Curatorial Work

In September 2019, Fornieles curated *Cursed Images* at Lisa Kandlhofer Gallery in Vienna, featuring 17 artists including John Bock, Chris Burden, VALIE EXPORT, Ann Hirsch, Rachel Maclean, Otto Muehl, Hermann Nitsch, Rudolf Schwarzkogler, David Wojnarowicz, and Issy Wood.

## Artistic Themes and Practice

Fornieles employs "film, social media platforms, sculpture, installation and performance to express the interaction of family, relationships, popular memes, language and the subcultures of the 21st century." His work operates within immersive simulations constructing alternative political and social spaces, typically involving cultural, social, and infrastructural production. His artistic practice centers on how narratives, habits, and technology shape social roles and personal identity formation.

## Personal Life

Fornieles was in a relationship with actress Felicity Jones from 2003 to 2013. He has lived and worked in London, Los Angeles, and Montreal.
