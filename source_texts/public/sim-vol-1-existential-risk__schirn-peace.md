# Sim Vol. 1: Existential Risk — Ed Fornieles, Sim Vol. 1: Existential Risk (SCHIRN PEACE)
_source: https://www.schirn-peace.org/en/post/ed-fornieles-sim-vol-1-existential-risk/ · accessed 2026-06-10_

**Title:** Ed Fornieles, Sim Vol. 1: Existential Risk
**Date:** 2017 · **Venue:** Schirn Kunsthalle Frankfurt (PEACE) · **Author:** Kristin Schrader

## Full text (as captured)

The artist Ed Fornieles (*1983, UK; lives and works in Montreal) created a
two-channel installation exploring existential themes through simulation and
role-play. The work opens with upbeat music contrasting apocalyptic imagery —
atomic clouds and alien-themed film posters — before transitioning to
testimonies from role players describing an epidemic scenario.

Fornieles employs what he describes as parasitic artistic strategies, drawing
from existing cultural systems and communication media. Previous works include
"New York New York Happy Happy" (2014), a performance at the New Museum where
participation levels correlated with monetary investment, and "Dorm Daze"
(2011), a Facebook-based sitcom using student profiles.

The installation presents cryptic narrative excerpts through dual projections —
one showing a role player's account, the other displaying 3D animations created
using forensic imaging techniques. A Game Book manual accompanies the work,
featuring sections titled "Emotional Confrontation," "A Last Walk," "No Escape,"
and "Death as Gateway," alongside an illustration labeled "Multidimensional
Being."

The work emphasizes experiencing multiple selves simultaneously during
gameplay. Rather than maintaining fictional boundaries, Fornieles integrates
personal relationships and emotional dimensions into simulated actions. Players'
accounts reveal profound themes: the necessity of rules for communal security,
confrontations with mortality, and questions of self-determination.

Scenario detail (per SCHIRN PEACE text): a sick person is visited and cared for
by co-players, even though the latter thereby put themselves at risk of
infection. A further player clubs a playing child to death from behind because
it puts the security of the community at risk. The work shifts from the danger
of dying in an epidemic into experiences of love and affection, emerging as a
discourse on peace and freedom of will and action.

## Key quotes
- "Peace is not the absence of conflict, but the ability to cope with it."
- "I am a thief, not a maker."
- "I am not interested in personal taste, and I don't like to judge — the more I
  can take myself out of the decision-making processes the better."
- "It is scary to think about all the potential that is lost by those who don't
  have the courage to unleash it."
