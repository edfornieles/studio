Twenty-One Hours

In September 2026, about 950 Claude agents set out on a search no human team would have had time to run. Over twenty-one hours they worked through more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, the enzymes that copy RNA into DNA, looking for anything unusual.

They narrowed the field to 3,500 candidate systems, then to twenty proposals for scientists to test. Human researchers in Anthropic's lab took the most promising one to the bench and expressed it. Anthropic reports it as a previously uncharacterized family of array-associated reverse transcriptases, arranged with repeats reminiscent of CRISPR.

What it does is still unknown. That is the point. The agents didn't replace the scientists; they widened the field of view, and the lab now has a new question to answer.

“What AlphaFold achieved is incredible. But what we're talking about here is different.” Jonah Cool, Anthropic

This is the story beneath the headlines about AI: steady, verifiable progress in labs, clinics and field hospitals, and the exploration it opens up.
