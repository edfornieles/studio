# Characterdate (Frame, Frieze London)

## Short description

Presented by Carlos/Ishikawa in the Frame section of Frieze Art Fair London, October 2012. Described as a "semi-fictional dating network" — a sculptural installation / participatory project continuing Fornieles' investigation of American college culture and online role-play, staged inside the commercial context of the art fair booth.

## Core question

Can the apparatus of an online dating network be staged as art inside an art fair — and what does matching "characters" rather than people reveal about identity and desire?

## Social machine

A (semi-fictional) dating network, run through the booth — casting and matching of characters, with assistants coordinating participants.

## Fictional device

The "Characterdate" dating network — a semi-fictional matchmaking system layered onto the Frieze booth, where participants engage as characters. (Fact: CV / press; Daisy Hogan bio confirms casting.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Online dating platforms; the art fair as a marketplace/meat-market; American college culture and its social rituals. (Fact + inference.)

## Audience / participant role

Fair visitors / recruited participants engaging the dating network as characters; a casting/coordination team managed them. (Fact: Daisy Hogan worked as assistant and casting coordinator.)

## Performer / subject role

Participants performing assigned characters within the dating-network fiction. TKTK — interview Ed for full mechanics.

## Emotional engine

Awkwardness, flirtation, and the comedy/discomfort of dating-as-performance in a commercial art setting. (Inference.)

## Ethical danger

Consent and exposure within a participatory dating fiction at a public fair; commodification of intimacy. (Inference.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That a "social network" can be instantiated as a temporary, located machine inside a host event (an art fair) rather than only online — the fair becomes the server. (Inference.)
