# The Dreamy Awards

## Short description

A fictional, immersive award ceremony staged as participatory performance with ~200+ people. Each attendee was assigned a character identity and a few plot points to enact, forming a 'three-tiered micro-society' of real public figures, the artist's collaborators and members of the public, all playing heightened versions of a self. Awards spanned music, TV, film, politics, business and technology; actor Zac Efron was a (pre-recorded) honoree, probing celebrity as a transferable construct.

## Core question

What is the cultural function of the awards ceremony as ritual, and how do power, status and identity form within a temporary scripted micro-society?

## Social machine

A participatory live-role-play event built on the award-show format/ritual; ticket purchase = entry into an assigned character.

## Fictional device

A wholly fictional award ceremony — "The Dreamy Awards" — honoring real public figures "helping to shape our current cultural and technological landscape" (music, TV, film, politics, business, technology). Each guest is given a fictional character identity, a profile of inflated traits, costume guidance and five pre-agreed plot points to enact, but the ending is undetermined. (Fact: Serpentine, Dazed, AnOther.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

The award ceremony as cultural ritual, and celebrity culture / image economy. Fornieles treats awards as celebrations that "reveal the priorities of the people doing the celebrating," and admits the bias: "The real reason why many of those people are winning those awards is because I like them." (Fact: AnOther.)

## Audience / participant role

From the moment of ticket purchase the audience member becomes a contributor: assigned a new identity for the evening, briefed by the artist (by profile and sometimes phone), and asked to enact five plot points while improvising freely — "your profile is just a starting point... there should be a feeling that you can shift and change when you want to." (Fact: Serpentine, AnOther, Dazed.)

## Performer / subject role

A "three-tiered micro-society": ~30% real public figures (from music, technology, advertising, film, TV, politics — including *Made in Chelsea* cast members), the artist's collaborators, and ~70% members of the public, all playing assigned characters as relative equals. Zac Efron is an honoree, appearing via a pre-recorded video clip. (Fact: AnOther, Dazed, Serpentine, Artlyst.)

## Emotional engine

Belonging and self-loss through collective role-play: Fornieles wanted "an atmosphere where people do feel this unity and they lose themselves in their characters," forging "social equality... through the shared experience of adopting a new character," and hoped role-play could open empathy — "you start using some of the processes of getting into character then you have the potential to turn left where you'd normally turn right." (Fact: AnOther, Dazed, Serpentine.)

## Ethical danger

Lower-stakes than NY NY HP HP (characters are consensually adopted at ticket purchase), but it instrumentalizes real celebrities (notably Efron) as raw material/honorees, and the artist's openly biased "I like them" curation flirts with using real people's images without their framing. (Inference grounded in AnOther/Dazed quotes.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: award ceremony, ritual, identity, role-play, participation, micro-society, celebrity, immersive theatre.

## Research method

Character design + light scripting at scale: writing per-attendee profiles with inflated traits, costume notes and five plot points, then briefing participants (profile + phone) and observing "the micro-society and bonds of power that form between them." The character is deliberately distanced from the person — "something that is far enough away from their personality for them to make distinct decisions." (Fact: Dazed, Serpentine.)

## Use of real material

Real public figures and celebrities as honorees/attendees (Zac Efron via pre-recorded video; *Made in Chelsea* cast and figures from music, fashion, film, TV, media), the real Serpentine Pavilion 2012 (Herzog & de Meuron with Ai Weiwei) as venue, and the real apparatus of an awards show. (Fact: Dazed, AnOther, Serpentine.)

## Use of fiction

The award show itself is invented; identities, profiles and plot points are fictional overlays. Fiction is consensual and additive — players know they are playing — rather than the hidden-actor deception of NY NY HP HP. (Fact: Serpentine, AnOther.)

## Emergent behaviour

Power and status bonds forming live between characters; the un-scripted ending; participants getting "lost" in roles. Designed explicitly to generate "a massive wealth of information and material" for later film/sculptural/video work. (Fact: Serpentine, AnOther.)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Filmed and presented as artwork on The Space (Arts Council England / BBC digital initiative; intended for BBC iPlayer), plus planned sculptural and video installations. Photographs by Lewis Ronald (Serpentine set). Images catalogued in image_manifests/public_gallery_manifest.jsonl (project: dreamy-awards); incoming manifest image_manifests/_incoming/dreamy-awards.jsonl. (Fact: Serpentine, AnOther, Dazed.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That a ritual format (the award show) plus per-person character profiles and a handful of plot points can manufacture a temporary society in which strangers, celebrities and the public meet as equals — and that this micro-society is itself the medium and the dataset. Role-play distance ("far enough away from their personality") is the lever for both empathy and emergence. (Inference grounded in sources.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

Captured sources do not record how cleanly the script held, whether honorees consented to/endorsed their "awards," or how the empathy aim played out in practice — open questions for interview. (Inference.)

## Possible mutations

Repeated/serial editions; online or distributed versions; reuse of the profile+plot-points engine for other rituals (galas — cf. NY NY HP HP, conferences, ceremonies); deeper integration with broadcast/streaming as the afterlife layer. (Inference.)

Serpentine Galleries Park Nights, Serpentine Pavilion 2012, London

**FACTS**
- Event: "The Dreamy Awards," 7 September 2012, Serpentine Gallery Pavilion 2012 (Herzog & de Meuron with Ai Weiwei), Hyde Park, London. Part of Park Nights; free admission. (Serpentine, Dazed)
- Premise: a fictional award ceremony honoring real figures shaping the "cultural and technological landscape" across music, TV, film, politics, business, technology. (Serpentine, Artlyst)
- Mechanic: every attendee assigned a character identity at ticket purchase; given a profile of inflated traits, costume notes and FIVE pre-agreed plot points; briefed via profile and sometimes phone; ending left open. (Serpentine, Dazed, AnOther)
- Structure: a "three-tiered micro-society" — ~30% real public figures (incl. *Made in Chelsea* cast; figures from music/tech/advertising/film/TV/politics), the artist's collaborators, and ~70% public. (AnOther, Dazed)
- Scale: "over 200" (Serpentine/Wikipedia/AnOther) to ~230 (Dazed) participants.
- Zac Efron: honoree, appearing via a pre-recorded video clip. (Serpentine, Dazed, Artlyst)
- Documentation: filmed for The Space (Arts Council England / BBC; intended for iPlayer), plus planned sculptural/video work. Photographer: Lewis Ronald. (Serpentine, AnOther)
- Quote (Fornieles, Dazed): "The process of character assigning tries to give the individual something that is far enough away from their personality for them to make distinct decisions."
- Quote (Fornieles on Efron, Dazed): "the Disney Corporation spent millions of pounds on him and his image, and what he's trying to do at the moment is trying to translate all those Disney dollars into his own, into something different."
- Quote (Fornieles, Dazed): "you start using some of the processes of getting into character then you have the potential to turn left where you'd normally turn right."
- Quote (Fornieles, AnOther): "your profile is just a starting point, and the plot points are just a starting point—there should be a feeling that you can shift and change when you want to."
- Quote (Fornieles, AnOther): "The real reason why many of those people are winning those awards is because I like them."
