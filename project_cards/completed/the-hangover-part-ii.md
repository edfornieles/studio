# The Hangover Part II

## Short description

An early solo exhibition of sculptural assemblage combining found objects, inflatables, polystyrene, building materials and consumer electronics — e.g. 'Contagion' (steel, inflatable rhino, polystyrene), 'ORCA' (inflatable whale, steel, peace flags), 'Monster' (Hardiplank, studwork, iPhone 3G), 'Glock' (socks, polystyrene, papier-mâché), and 'Dorm Daze' / 'Adventureland' installations. Channels US teen/party-movie iconography and the residue of Animal House and Dorm Daze into post-party sculptural debris.

## Core question

How do the mythologies and detritus of American teen/party/college culture translate into sculptural form?

## Social machine

Object-based sculpture/installation drawing on party-movie franchises and reusing the residue of earlier performance works rather than a participatory system.

## Fictional device

The exhibition borrows the title of a Hollywood sequel (The Hangover Part II) and treats the gallery as the "morning after" of a fiction — the literal detritus left behind by the earlier Animal House performance, restaged as sculpture. (Inference) The sequel-title device frames the show as the second instalment of an ongoing fictional universe rather than a discrete object show.

## Real-world system it attaches to

Hollywood teen/party-movie franchises (The Hangover, Animal House, Dorm Daze, Adventureland) and US college/fraternity mythology; consumer and media culture (iPhone 3G, Twitter feed, Spiderman/Scooby-Doo merchandise, comics) folded into the works as material.

## Audience / participant role

Gallery viewer / reader of residue. Where the source performances (Animal House, Dorm Daze) made participants into performers, this show recasts the audience as forensic visitors picking through the aftermath. (Inference from the "detritus / morning after" framing.)

## Performer / subject role

No live performers in the gallery; the "performers" are displaced into the residue — the work _The Hangover_ is literally "carpet, Animal House residue," and _Dorm Daze_ and _Adventureland_ incorporate photographs, a script book and props from those participatory works. The earlier participants survive only as material trace. (Fact: materials lists. Inference: the role-displacement reading.)

## Emotional engine

The comedown — anticlimax, exhaustion and faint menace after the party. Inflatable rhino, whale and party debris read as both festive and abject; the title promises raucousness but the room delivers wreckage. (Inference.)

## Ethical danger

The works reuse documentation and material generated by real participants in the Facebook-based Dorm Daze (real photos, scalped-profile material) as gallery objects; the danger is the same bleed between real lives and saleable fiction that the participatory works opened. (Inference, grounded in the materials lists and the Dorm Daze sourcing.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: American college culture, party movies, frat/Animal House, found objects, inflatables, assemblage, consumer detritus, residue.

## Research method

Harvesting and recombination: collecting the physical and digital residue of prior performances and consumer culture, then assembling it into sculpture. Found objects (inflatables, building materials, sportswear, soft toys, devices) are combined with literal performance leftovers. (Inference from materials lists.)

## Use of real material

Extensive and literal: Animal House residue (in _The Hangover_), Dorm Daze photographs and script book (in _Adventureland_ and the _Dorm Daze_ work), a working Twitter feed and computers, an iPhone 3G, a Spiderman costume, comics and merchandise. Real media artefacts are embedded as raw matter.

## Use of fiction

The Hollywood-sequel title and franchise references provide a fictional frame that organises otherwise disparate junk into a coherent "world." The objects gesture at an off-stage narrative (a party that already happened) the viewer never witnesses.

## Emergent behaviour

Not a live emergent system like Dorm Daze; here the "emergence" is retrospective — meaning accreted around residue once it was removed from its original context and re-presented as sculpture. (Inference.)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

This show is itself the afterlife of earlier works: it converts the documentation of Animal House and Dorm Daze into permanent, saleable sculpture. Installation and work images are catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/the-hangover-part-ii.jsonl (from carlosishikawa.com). A publication accompanied the show (downloadable from the gallery site per the exhibition page).

## What this project teaches the studio

- A performance's residue can be re-authored as a second, object-based work — documentation is raw material, not just record.
- A borrowed franchise title can do heavy framing work, situating a show as a "sequel" inside an ongoing fictional universe.
- The comedown/aftermath is as productive an emotional register as the event itself.

## What failed or remained unresolved

No press-release prose was located online, so the artist's stated intentions for the sculptures remain under-documented (the conceptual readings above are inference). To confirm with Ed: how deliberate the "morning after" framing was, and whether selling participants' residue as objects raised consent questions. (`needs_review`)

## Possible mutations

- A show staged explicitly as "Part III" — the residue of this residue.
- Re-presenting the same objects with the full participant narratives restored as wall text.
- A version where the residue is digital-only (the Twitter feed / accounts) rather than physical.

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Treat the residue of any performance as a candidate for a second work; ask what its object-form would be.
- When reusing participant-generated material, flag consent/rights early (see [[dorm-daze]] ethical danger).
- A franchise/sequel title is a cheap, powerful framing device — propose one when a body of work needs a world.
