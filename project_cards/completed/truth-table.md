# Truth Table

## Short description

A pornographic VR work presented as a "new VR product that allows the viewer to
occupy a changing host of bodies as they engage in a chain of sexual
interactions." Roughly every 20 seconds the bodies, sexual positions and
environments switch, with variables such as race, body type and age given equal
emphasis. Fornieles' first solo show in Italy; part of his simulation strand
using combinatorial/randomized systems to generate experience. (Fact: CURA. /
Basement Roma texts.)

## Core question

What happens to desire and identity when erotic experience is generated by a
randomized combinatorial system that refuses to learn your preferences? The work
sets itself explicitly *against* the recommendation logic of social media —
rather than reinforcing a "filter bubble," it equalises every variable. (Fact:
press text. Framing as a question is inference.)

## Social machine

A single-user VR experience driven by a "truth table" of variables that are
re-rolled at equal probability on a ~20-second cycle, paired with a physical bed
/ VR-set interface in the gallery. Where platform algorithms converge on a user's
predilections, this machine deliberately diverges from them. (Fact: ~20s cycle,
equal distribution, bed/VR set; "truth table" framing is the work's own logic.)

## Fictional device

The conceit of a consumer "VR product" — the gallery is "converted into the
provider of an experience," staging the work as if it were a commercial sexual
technology you could buy and plug into. The viewer is cast as a customer/user of
a near-future onanism device. (Inference from "new VR product" framing; confirm
with Ed.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

VR/immersive technology; online pornography; recommendation and content-ranking
algorithms; "technologies of sexuality." The press text names "algorithmic
content models, immersive virtual realities, and technologies of sexuality" as
the work's subject. (Fact.)

## Audience / participant role

A solo VR wearer who inhabits a shifting succession of bodies and viewpoints —
becoming, in turn, each party to the sexual chain. The experience "implicates
viewers" while reading as dream-like and disjointed rather than coherent.
(Fact: occupies "a changing host of bodies"; dream-state framing from CURA.)

## Performer / subject role

There are no live performers; the "performers" are the randomized rendered
bodies and the system's reshuffling logic. The wearer is both subject and
spectator of their own substituted body. (Inference.)

## Emotional engine

Disorientation, jarring, and the erotic uncanny: by stripping away familiar,
preference-reinforced patterns, the randomization produces "jarring" moments and
a dream-like derangement of desire. (Fact: "jarring," "dream-like" in source;
the affective reading is inference.)

## Ethical danger

Pornographic VR content and the politics of bodies/identity rendered as
interchangeable, equalised variables — "enforced non-discriminatory shagging,
the politicisation of onanism" (phrase from coverage of the related Seed VR
work). Equal representation of race/body/age can read as either anti-bias or as
flattening real difference into a slot machine. (Fact: quoted phrase re: Seed VR;
the tension is inference. Confirm Ed's intent.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: VR, simulation, pornography, randomization, combinatorics, desire, the
body, filter bubble, onanism. Installation in coverage paired the VR set/bed with
wall panels described by a reviewer (of the Seed VR work) as "kinky John
Baldessaris."

## Research method

Systems-building: define a parameter space (bodies, positions, environments,
demographic variables) and a re-roll rule (equal probability, ~20s), then let the
combinatorics "play themselves out." Aligns with Fornieles' stated practice — "I
set up a bunch of parameters; then people, images or algorithms play themselves
out" (Le Random). (Fact: quote; mapping to Truth Table is inference.)

## Use of real material

The vocabulary and UX of commercial VR, online porn, and recommendation systems —
the "VR product," the customer framing, the demographic variables that
ranking-algorithms actually optimise over. (Inference grounded in press text.)

## Use of fiction

Staging the gallery as a product showroom for a fictional consumer sex-VR device;
the "truth table" as a quasi-logical fiction governing desire. (Inference.)

## Emergent behaviour

The meaning is produced by the combinatorial churn rather than authored scenes:
equalised variables collide into configurations the viewer would never have
selected, so novelty/jarring/dream-logic *emerges* from the rule-set. This is the
"terrifyingly emergent" quality Fornieles attributes to his systems. (Inference +
Le Random quote.)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Installation photos by Roberto Apa (Basement Roma) and a parallel set on CURA.
(ED-BR series), catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/truth-table.jsonl. The
body-shifting VR premise reappears within [[seed]] (Carlos/Ishikawa, London,
22 Sep – 28 Oct 2017) as a VR-porn program; likely the same or a closely related
work re-presented. (Fact: photos/venue; equivalence is inference — see notes.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That a system can be built to oppose, rather than serve, the user's revealed
preferences — designing for divergence and equal emphasis instead of
convergence. A model for using randomization as a political/aesthetic stance
against recommendation culture. (Inference.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

Whether "equal emphasis" reads as critique of algorithmic bias or as its own
flattening of difference; whether the dream-like incoherence lands as insight or
just noise. Documentation is thin (no extended interview text located).
(Inference / research gap.)

## Possible mutations

Multi-user version; a "truth table" applied to non-erotic content (news,
relationships, work); exposing the live variable table as an interface; pairing
with [[sim-vol-1-existential-risk]]'s role-play so randomized bodies acquire
narrated moral stakes. (Inference.)

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

When Ed describes a system, ask for its parameter space and its re-roll/scoring
rule explicitly. Flag when a "neutral" or "equal" design choice carries a
political reading. Treat randomization as an authored stance, not an absence of
authorship. (Inference.)

## Research dossier (web, 2026-06-10)

**Confirmed facts**
- Title: *Truth Table*; Fornieles' first solo show in Italy. (CURA., Basement Roma)
- Venue/dates: Basement Roma, Viale Mazzini 128, Rome; **26 Oct – 23 Dec 2016**.
- Text by **Dorothy Howard**; photos by **Roberto Apa**; "All images courtesy the artist."
- Mechanic: "a new VR product that allows the viewer to occupy a changing host of
  bodies as they engage in a chain of sexual interactions"; variables change
  **~every 20 seconds** (bodies, positions, environments); race/body type/age
  **equally represented**.
- Stated intent: pushes "against the re-enforcing bubble; instead, equally
  distributed variables work to counteract expectations and predilections";
  "negotiates the psychosomatic forms of relationality implied by algorithmic
  content models, immersive virtual realities, and technologies of sexuality."
- Reception framing: experience described as **dream-like / jarring**, not
  scientifically rigorous.

**Method quote (Le Random, 2026 interview, applies across the strand)**
- "I set up a bunch of parameters; then people, images or algorithms play
  themselves out."
- "At its best it's terrifyingly emergent."

**Relationship to Seed (flagged uncertain)**
- Carlos/Ishikawa lists *Truth Table* (2016, Rome), *Sim Vol. 1* (2017, NY) and
  *Seed* (London, **22 Sep – 28 Oct 2017**) as separate solo shows. (Fact)
- A *Seed* work was described as "a VR porn program where the body of your lover
  constantly shifts gender, race and shape" — same body-shifting premise as
  *Truth Table*. **Inference:** the *Truth Table* VR and the *Seed* VR component
  are the same or a closely related work re-shown. Exact equivalence, and the
  precise relationship between the 2016 and 2017 datings, are **not confirmed** —
  ask Ed.
