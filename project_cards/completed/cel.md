# Cel

## Short description

Documents a 72-hour immersive live-action role-play (LARP) in which ten participants inhabit an embodied, fictional version of extremist/'incel' online communities (largely white men), living in a contained "cell" installation built in the studio under dominance/submission protocols drawn from 4chan and Reddit. Developed with Nordic LARP designer Nina Runa Essendrop. Stage one ran on the protocol "you must always have someone lower than yourself"; stage two staged a fictional mass-shooting/suicide that collapsed the hierarchy and opened a space of "abstract play" where alternative social structures could emerge. Recorded on chest cams and CCTV; the resulting two-channel film mimics grainy street-violence/YouTube fight footage and is interleaved with participant debrief interviews. Exhibited at Carlos/Ishikawa (London) and FUTURA (Prague), with panels (e.g. 'Post-Patriarchal Masculinity'; 'Male identity, the alt-right and violent extremism') and a "Cel – Debrief" podcast.

## Core question

What ideologies produce aggressive/extremist masculinity, and can embodied role-play generate a "critical empathy" that helps render those structures visible and dismantle them — from the inside, rather than from an outer critical perspective?

## Social machine

A protocol-driven, two-stage Nordic-LARP simulation of extremist online male communities, run for 72 hours in a sealed installation, documented via surveillance/body cameras and exhibited as a two-channel video installation plus public panels and a debrief podcast. The "machine" is the rule-set itself — hierarchy enforced by the protocol that there must always be someone lower than you — plus the Nordic safety apparatus (define-limits workshops, the OK symbol, "break", "cut", buddy-system debrief) that makes extreme play survivable.

## Fictional device

A fictional radicalised alt-right gamer group on a program of "self-betterment" that culminates in a planned mass murder-suicide as their "ultimate revenge against the world." Each participant plays a named character (Sam, Troy, Chad, Matt, David, Eli, Jon, Slayer, Tom, Max); Fornieles plays "Eli." The characters and their belief-worlds are composites scraped from 4chan/Reddit personality profiles — black-pill ideology, neoliberal "natural selection" affirmations, Jordan-Peterson-style self-help, Stacy/Chad mythology. The mass-shooting is enacted symbolically, not literally, as a narrative hinge that detonates the hierarchy.

## Real-world system it attaches to

Online radicalisation; incel / black-pill / alt-right imageboards (4chan, Reddit); the pop-psychology and neoreactionary self-help economy (Jordan Peterson); the genre of "spectacular" mass-shooter manifestos (Auvinen, Columbine's Eric Harris and the "Natural Selection" t-shirt); and, per Franco 'Bifo' Berardi, the "suicidal form of the neoliberal will to win." Underneath all of it: patriarchy understood via bell hooks as a non-gender-specific "will to dominate."

## Audience / participant role

Two audiences. (1) The ten *players*, who agree in advance to inhabit hyper-aggressive personas and to endure/perform real physical aggression (forced shaving, humiliation, waterboarding — Fornieles was himself waterboarded in play) under consent protocols. Participants reportedly included actors, Nordic-LARP club members, young male gamers, and a woman playing a male dominatrix character. (2) The gallery *viewer*, positioned as a witness to surveillance-style footage and debriefs — implicated rather than comforted, asked to recognise the patriarchal structures they too inhabit.

## Performer / subject role

Fornieles is simultaneously game-designer (with Essendrop), player ("Eli"), and subject of his own analysis. He explicitly refuses the safe critical distance: "Far from distancing himself from the value systems that underpin incel culture, Fornieles became a distinct part of them." His character Eli survives by "simply giving up the need for personal or social bonds" and turning to the leader and the group for worth — a deliberate enactment of patriarchal detachment.

## Emotional engine

Critical empathy through "bleed." Nordic LARP's concept of bleed (bleed-in: the player's state colours the character; bleed-out: the character's state colours the player; memetic bleed: ideas/memes pass between the two) is the engine. The work runs on the discomfort of feeling, from the inside, the pull of belonging, validation, detachment and revenge — and then, in the debrief, the slow "waves" of making sense of it. The studio's own affective stake: Fornieles ties Cel to his memory of a rural English state school "battle royal" and to repression as a survival "tool."

## Ethical danger

Acute and named by the artist: "in many ways, Cel effectively replicates the structures that it seeks to dismantle." Risks include real psychological/physical harm to players; aestheticising or glamorising mass-shooter ideology and misogyny; the possibility that "critical empathy" reads as sympathy. Reception was mixed for exactly these reasons. The safety architecture (workshops, limits-mapping, OK/break/cut, months-long buddy-system debrief) is the studio's answer, but the danger is structural, not accidental.

## Research method

First-person immersion as research. Pre-production: months of character development and play-testing workshops where participants' limits were physically mapped (e.g. testing burning wax on skin). Source material harvested from 4chan/Reddit and theorised through bell hooks, Audre Lorde, Franco Berardi (*Heroes: Mass Murder and Suicide*) and analysis of Jordan Peterson. The exhibition booklet is a co-authored text with writer/curator Tamara Hart. Post-immersion debrief interviews became both safety practice and the structuring material of the film.

## Use of real material

Real online subcultural content (4chan/Reddit profiles, manifestos, memes — including a Gillette "We Believe" meme), real theory, and real bodies undergoing real (consented) physical aggression. The film is built from real CCTV/body-cam footage and real debrief testimony. The studio insists the film "can only ever be an interpretation of ten deeply personal experiences."

## Use of fiction

Fiction as a safe-enough container for the unspeakable: named fictional characters, a fictional self-improvement-to-massacre narrative arc, and a symbolic (not literal) mass murder-suicide that lets the group's hierarchy be destroyed and re-imagined. Fiction here is the "abstract play" space entered after the staged shooting, where alternative, non-dominance-based social structures can be improvised.

## Emergent behaviour

The protocol "always have someone lower than yourself" was designed to *generate* hierarchy emergently through interaction; it did. Aggression, alliance, and humiliation self-organised. The deliberately under-defined post-shooting phase was designed for emergence of alternatives. Bleed produced unscripted emotional aftermath that unfolded "in waves" over months — the real, uncontrollable output of the system.

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Two-channel film installation; exhibition booklet (co-authored with Tamara Hart); "Cel – Debrief" podcast; two public panels (3 April and 17 April 2019); installation/still images catalogued in image_manifests/public_gallery_manifest.jsonl (project: cel). Toured: Carlos/Ishikawa London (Mar–Apr 2019) → FUTURA Prague (May–Jun 2019, curated by Christina Gigliotti) → screening + conversation with Rhizome / New Museum, New York (Dec 2019). Critical afterlife includes Eddy Frankel's 4/5 Time Out review, Matt Colquhoun's "unconsciousness raising" essay, and the New Models "MANPLAY" podcast.

## What this project teaches the studio

That fiction + protocol + a robust consent architecture can let people *inhabit* an ideology rather than merely discuss it — and that inhabiting renders the ideology's "conceptual architecture" visible in a way theory cannot. That the designer should be inside the system, implicated, not above it ("Reality is just bad LARP design": if we design reality as we design LARP, alternative social structures can emerge). And that safety is not a disclaimer bolted on but the load-bearing mechanic of any high-intensity participatory work: limits-mapping, the OK/break/cut vocabulary, and a long debrief are what make extremity ethical.

## What failed or remained unresolved

By the artist's own admission, Cel risks reproducing the very structures it critiques. Time Out judged the video over-long and the psychological exegesis "a little over-egged." Critics asked whether "critical empathy" is distinguishable from sympathy, and whether simulating alt-right communities accomplishes anything beyond opening "new terrains of dialogue" (the artist's modest claim). The bigger question Colquhoun pressed — what it means to use technology to actively deconstruct the self, and whether one could *design* platforms that do so constructively — Fornieles "did not have an immediate answer" to. Long-term effects on participants are acknowledged as unfolding over months and not fully knowable.

## Possible mutations

- A debrief-only work: stage the months of after-talk, not the violence.
- An assistant/agent version: an AI that role-plays a hostile ideology under strict consent protocols so a user can practise "critical empathy" safely.
- Inverting the protocol: a LARP whose rule is "you must always raise someone above yourself," to study emergent solidarity.
- Designing the "alternative social structures" of the post-shooting phase as a standalone platform (per Babble / Truth Table).

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Treat consent and safety vocabulary as core mechanics, not footnotes: always offer an explicit "cut" / off-ramp before entering distressing or adversarial role-play, and debrief after.
- When asked to inhabit a hostile worldview for understanding, aim for *critical empathy* (render its logic visible) — never advocacy; flag the danger of replication out loud.
- Speak from a located, vulnerable, first-person register where appropriate rather than a falsely neutral "objective" voice; the studio distrusts the disembodied authoritative voice.
- Never glamorise or operationalise violence; keep enactment symbolic and bounded.

**Two-stage structure (fact, booklet).** (1) "Survival of the fittest," protocol *you must always have someone lower than yourself*. (2) "Mass shooting and suicide" — two characters kill peers then themselves, collapsing the hierarchy into "abstract play."

**Patriarchy framing (quote, booklet).** "My understanding of patriarchy is based on bell hooks' notion of the will to dominate." Also: "Far from distancing himself from the value systems that underpin incel culture, Fornieles became a distinct part of them ... Rather than sympathising with these misogynist, alt-right, male groups, Cel proposes a mode of critical empathy to engage with them through play."

**Self-critique (quote, booklet).** "It can be argued that little is accomplished in simulating alt-right, hyper-masculine communities. Indeed, in many ways, Cel effectively replicates the structures that it seeks to dismantle. However, the key value of Cel lies in its ability to open new terrains of dialogue."

**Theory cited (fact, booklet).** bell hooks (will to dominate); Audre Lorde (*Your Silence Will Not Protect You*); Franco 'Bifo' Berardi (*Heroes: Mass Murder and Suicide*, Verso 2015 — "suicidal form of the Neoliberal will to win"); Jordan Peterson (analysed as "the ultimate neoconservative daddy"); Pekka-Eric Auvinen manifesto; Columbine's Eric Harris "NATURAL SELECTION" t-shirt.

**Safety mechanics (fact, booklet).** Pre-immersion limits-mapping workshops (e.g. testing wax on skin); non-verbal "OK symbol"; "break" (lower intensity) and "cut" (stop immediately); buddy-system debrief continuing over months. Fornieles states he was waterboarded in play and used the OK symbol.

**Press / reception.** Eddy Frankel, *Time Out* (27 Mar 2019), 4/5: the work is "halfway between something like the Stanford Prison Experiment and a particularly nasty episode of 'Big Brother'"; footage mirrors YouTube street fights; the incel-psychology explanation is "a little over-egged" but viewers leave "deeply aware that male anger and aggression exist." Matt Colquhoun (xenogothic), 2 Aug 2019: links Cel to *Dorm Daze* as forms of "Artaudian consciousness raising" / "unconsciousness raising"; reports Fornieles "described Cel as a sort of inversion of 'second-wave feminist discourses'" and "did not have an immediate answer" to whether one could design platforms that deconstruct the performative self. New Models podcast EP 8 "MANPLAY" features Fornieles discussing the work.
