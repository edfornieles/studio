# Workland: the fence is a narrow place

## Short description

A 2015 exhibition by Ed Fornieles (with Hannah Black and Matt Goerzen) at Château Shatto, Los Angeles (12 Sep – 14 Nov 2015), that turned the gallery into a communal workspace — "a hive of individuals with self-ascribed tasks" — where visitors watch "people gently performing themselves working." (Fact, per gallery statement.)

## Core question

What happens to "work" when it is detached from output and staged as atmosphere? The statement compares the gallery to "the scene at your nearest café, where you may surround oneself with the atmosphere of productivity to help fuel your own" — so the question is about productivity as a contagious mood and a performance of self, rather than labour as production. (Inference grounded in the verbatim statement; exact framing TKTK — interview Ed.)

## Social machine

The gallery is reformatted as an open-plan office / co-working floor populated by individuals carrying out "self-ascribed tasks." Visitors become onlookers to other people's performed busyness — productivity as spectacle and as a feedback loop (the café effect: being among workers makes you feel like working). (Fact + inference from statement.)

## Fictional device

The fiction is the "workplace" itself: the gallery is dressed as a worksite and its occupants as workers with invented jobs, collapsing the line between an art installation and an actual office. Fornieles' object-titles borrow recent film/TV/streaming titles ("The Man in the High Castle," "Maze Runner: The Scorch Trials," "The Last Witch Hunter," "A Walk in the Woods"), folding pop-cultural content into the work-fiction. (Fact: titles confirmed on page; intended use TKTK — interview Ed.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Contemporary labour and the aesthetics of productivity: the post-industrial / creative-class workplace, co-working and café-working culture, self-branding, and "performing oneself working." Goerzen's contribution attaches it to **security** systems (security-themed adhesive vinyl decals/posters). (Fact: media confirmed; reading is inference.)

## Audience / participant role

Observer of a worksite — placed in the position of someone walking past an office or sitting in a productivity-saturated café. The visitor is invited to absorb (and possibly catch) the atmosphere of productivity rather than to consume discrete artworks. (Inference from statement.)

## Performer / subject role

The occupants "gently perform themselves working" — i.e. they enact a low-key, naturalistic version of labour for an audience. How staffed/scripted this was (paid performers, the artists, gallery staff, durational vs. fixed) is not documented. TKTK — interview Ed.

## Emotional engine

Low-grade ambient pressure and recognition: the familiar, slightly uncanny pull of other people's busyness; the comedy and unease of productivity-as-mood. (Inference; not stated in sources.)

## Ethical danger

Aestheticising labour — turning work, precarity and self-optimisation into a viewable atmosphere risks flattening real working conditions into décor. The collaboration with Goerzen's security imagery hints at surveillance/control undertones. (Inference; not named in sources — interview Ed.)

## Aesthetic world

Office/worksite minimalism crossed with post-internet materials: digital prints in aluminium frames, polished aluminium constructions, video, mixed-media installation (Fornieles); steel-and-latex sculpture and video, e.g. "Positive Visualization 1" (Hannah Black); security decals in adhesive vinyl (Matt Goerzen). Tags: work, systems, productivity, office, security, post-internet, installation. (Fact.)

## Research method

Not documented. Likely: assembling a cast/scenario and a set of props that read as "office," plus pop-culture-titled objects. TKTK — interview Ed.

## Use of real material

Real materials of the workplace and of productivity culture (the co-working/café template; security signage as a real visual genre). Object titles lifted directly from real, contemporaneous films/series. (Fact + inference.)

## Use of fiction

The over-arching fiction is that the gallery is a functioning workplace and its people are workers with jobs — a staged "Workland." (Fact, per statement; depth of scripting TKTK.)

## Emergent behaviour

The "café effect" the statement describes — visitors potentially absorbing and mirroring the performed productivity — is the intended emergent dynamic. Whether this actually occurred is not documented. TKTK — interview Ed.

## What this project teaches the studio

That a social machine can be built almost entirely out of *atmosphere* — productivity as a transmissible mood — and that "performing oneself working" is itself a legible role the audience recognises and can be pulled into. Useful precedent for staging everyday systems as low-key, naturalistic live environments. (Inference.)

## Possible mutations

A remote/Zoom-era "Workland"; a co-working space whose only product is the feeling of productivity; a worksite where the surveillance layer (Goerzen) is made primary. (Studio inference, not from sources.)
