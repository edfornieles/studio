# Cursed Images

## Short description

A group exhibition **curated by Ed Fornieles** at Galerie Kandlhofer (Lisa Kandlhofer), Vienna, 17 September – 11 October 2019, part of Vienna's "curated by 2019" festival (theme: "Circulation"). It takes its title and premise from the internet "cursed images" genre — images that feel "illogical, abnormal or wrong in some fundamental way" — and stages it as a show about images that transmit dread on contact. Fornieles' role here is **curator, not exhibiting artist.** (Fact.)

## Core question

Can the "cursed image" — an everyday picture that nonetheless unsettles, confuses or disturbs the viewer on contact — be treated as a real category of image with its own logic, and what happens when you concentrate many of them in one room? Fornieles frames the curse as both an affliction and "a cure, allowing us to see the borders of our expectations, a hidden desire for harmony." (Fact: from his curatorial statement.)

## Social machine

A curated group show as a transmission device: images selected so that the unease "passes to the viewer on contact." Fornieles describes a two-stage exposure — first the discrete jolt of individual cursed images, then a second stage in which "the curse bleeds out into your everyday world" and "inanimate objects begin to develop their own agency." The exhibition is the apparatus that incubates this contagion. (Fact, per statement.)

## Fictional device

The animating fiction is that a cursed image literally carries "a demonic spirit that inhabits certain images and which passes to the viewer on contact." This quasi-supernatural framing — image as carrier of a transmissible curse — is the conceit organising an otherwise art-historically diverse checklist. (Fact: verbatim from statement.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Internet image culture and its native genres — specifically the "cursed images" meme lineage seeded by the Tumblr account cursedimages.tumblr.com (from ~2015), which began defining and categorising images with "a disruptive and unsettling effect." It links that online genre to a longer art lineage: Viennese Actionism (Nitsch, Muehl, Schwarzkogler), VALIE EXPORT, body/abjection art (Wojnarowicz), and contemporary post-internet practice. (Fact.)

## Audience / participant role

Viewer-as-host/carrier: the visitor is positioned as the surface the curse "passes to… on contact," meant to feel "confusion, unease or dread" and, ideally, to carry the effect out into the everyday afterwards. (Fact + light inference from statement.)

## Performer / subject role

There are no live performers; the "subjects" are the works and their makers. The curated artists function as suppliers of cursed images across media — painting, sculpture, video, photography, installation, performance documentation. (Inference from format.)

## Emotional engine

Dread, unease, confusion, the uncanny — Fornieles explicitly invokes **Freud's uncanny** ("a strangeness in the ordinary") and **Lacan**, citing the curse as placing us "in the field where we do not know how to distinguish bad and good, pleasure from displeasure." Discomfort is the intended affect, with a redemptive turn (the curse as "cure"). (Fact.)

## Ethical danger

Assembling deliberately disturbing material (Actionist abjection, body horror, transgressive imagery) and presenting "the curse" as desirable risks aestheticising genuine distress or trauma, and risks the second-stage "bleed" being experienced as real harm rather than insight. The show courts discomfort by design. (Inference; not flagged as such in sources.)

## Aesthetic world

A deliberately heterogeneous, art-historically promiscuous hang: Viennese Actionism and 1970s feminist/body art beside post-internet and meme-native work. Named works include VALIE EXPORT, "Zyklus zur Zivilisation…" (1972); Hermann Nitsch, "Schüttbild mit Malhemd" (2011); Otto Muehl, "Zwei Ziegen" (1984); Rachel Maclean, "Mending a Face II–IV" / "The Second/Third Stage of Mending a Face" (2019); Anna Uddenberg, "Disconnect (airplane mode)" (2018); John Bock, "Hell's Bells" (2017); Issy Wood, "WASP" / "Fretting Dracula" (2018); Puppies Puppies, "Resusci Anne, CPR…" (2019); Omsk Social Club, "The Memetic Zone" (2019); plus Chris Burden, Ann Hirsch, Joey Holder, Rudolf Schwarzkogler, Tommy Turner, David Wojnarowicz, Doug Bressler, "Naked," and Katerina Zbortkova. Tags: cursed images, internet/meme image culture, the uncanny, abjection, Viennese Actionism, dread, curation. (Fact.)

## Research method

Curatorial: Fornieles describes years of personally "collecting and spreading" cursed images, then theorising the genre (Tumblr taxonomy → Freud → Lacan) and translating that taxonomy into a physical group show. (Fact, per statement.)

## Use of real material

Real existing artworks (spanning ~1972–2019) and the real online genre of "cursed images" (cursedimages.tumblr.com), plus the real critical apparatus of Freud and Lacan. (Fact.)

## Use of fiction

The framing fiction — the "demonic spirit" / transmissible curse and the second-stage "bleed" where objects gain agency — is the speculative overlay that turns a survey into a haunted system. (Fact.)

## Emergent behaviour

The intended emergent effect is cumulative: dread compounding across the room until "the curse bleeds out into your everyday world." Whether visitors actually reported this is not documented. TKTK — interview Ed.

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Well documented on Galerie Kandlhofer's site (overview, works, and installation-view pages); also covered by GalleriesNow and the Nitsch Foundation. Eight installation-view image URLs (artlogic CDN, clearly captioned) are catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/cursed-images.jsonl. (Fact.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That an internet-native affect (the "cursed image") can be formalised into a real curatorial/theoretical category and a transmission machine — and that pairing meme logic with deep art history (Actionism, the uncanny) lends a born-online sensibility art-historical weight. A model for treating *contagion of feeling* as the exhibition's true medium. (Inference.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

Not documented; no critical account of shortcomings was found. Whether the "second stage" bleed-out actually landed for audiences is unverified. TKTK — interview Ed.

## Possible mutations

A purely online "cursed images" exhibition; a version that tracks the physiological/affective response of viewers; extending the "curse as cure" thesis into a therapeutic or ritual format. (Studio inference, not from sources.)
