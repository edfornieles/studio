# Associations

## Short description

A body of work made during the first Covid-19 lockdown in which Fornieles collected images from the internet and connected them one to the next by association — formal, symbolic or cultural — building long chains (e.g. hand → family → house → globe; globe → dinosaur; hand → Tony Stark/Robert Downey Jr). It exists as a roughly 40-minute moving-image work with a free-associated voiceover, as a sequential Instagram feed (shown by HeK Basel, 8 Jun – 7 Jul 2021), and at Carlos/Ishikawa (17 Apr – 22 May 2021) as large-format inkjet prints with multi-aperture window mounts that arrange the images as interlocking, crossword-like "maps." A published rule-set/protocol lets others play the game. The chains map Fornieles' own tastes, biases and subconscious against the wider, repetition-bound landscape of online image culture.

## Core question

How is identity formed, replicated and sustained through associative chains of images — and what do those chains reveal about personal bias, cultural algorithms and the limits of the "free" associative mind? When you follow your own associations to exhaustion, do you discover freedom or "a map of your limitations"?

## Social machine

An associative image-chaining engine: internet images linked by resemblance into navigable networks, mediated by reverse-image search (Fornieles found Yandex retrieved his desired images most efficiently). Shown as layered physical print-maps, a voiced film, and a sequential social-media feed. The protocol is published so the "machine" can be run by others, revealing a "shared web of associations."

## Fictional device

A semi-fictional, ultra-honest first-person performance of the artist's own stream of consciousness: Fornieles "performs himself," hollowing himself out as a voiceover narrator over the image chain, so the work reads as "a broken map of my subconscious." (Inference: the device is autofiction/automatism rather than a separate fictional character — closer to psychoanalytic free association than to LARP, though the artist links it to his role-play practice.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Online image culture; reverse-image search and recommendation algorithms (Google, Bing, Yandex); social-media feeds and infinite scroll; the attention economy; and, behind these, the human-labelled training datasets and "cultural algorithms" that decide which images resemble which. Also psychoanalysis (free association) and the NFT/crypto-art moment of 2021.

## Audience / participant role

The viewer becomes an immersed scroller, "absorbed into the mesmerising chain of images, losing yourself within the maze" (Artvisor). Because the protocol is published, the audience can also become a participant/operator, running their own associative chains and so confronting "unseen biases that are baked into our environments." Burbridge frames the ideal mode as "attentive, meditative" — immersion doubling as awareness.

## Performer / subject role

Fornieles is both performer and subject: the voiceover is his, the associations are his, and the work deliberately "point[s] back to me as a person" rather than retreating into a "bodiless objectivity." He frames this as refusing the "white male position… the unstated voice of history, news, facts, and authority," giving up the director's control in favour of an emergent system.

## Emotional engine

Compulsion, exhaustion and exposure. Made from inside a "soft zone state" of lockdown illness and "compulsive behaviour, Netflix, social media, pornography," the work runs on the addictive pull of the frictionless feed while smuggling in "hidden nasties… applying pressure." Emotionally it trades on confession, vulnerability and the involuntary triggering of personal memory by images ("no image or thought is a dead-end").

## Ethical danger

The work foregrounds algorithmic and cultural bias — "image searches… are insanely biased towards certain aesthetics and demographics" — and risks reproducing the very biases it exposes by aestheticising the scroll. There is also the danger of self-exposure (the "ultra-honest mode," experiments with MDMA to break inhibition) and of the work being misread as "merely… something about image culture, something purely conceptual" rather than personal. (Inference, grounded in the CURA interview.)

## Research method

Daily, near-automatic practice over several months in lockdown: place a digital image, connect it to the next by formal/symbolic/cultural association, repeat. Sourcing via reverse-image search (Yandex most effective). First film edit from a "very messy stream of consciousness" (briefly with MDMA, soon abandoned), then edited and added to. The generative rules/protocol were published. Method explicitly likened to psychoanalytic association (Fornieles began psychoanalysis around this time) and to his earlier role-play works (*Dorm Daze*, *Cel*).

## Use of real material

Real internet images, collected via real search engines; real personal memories surfaced in the voiceover; the artist's own biography and subconscious as raw material; cited intellectual material in the accompanying Ben Burbridge essay and CURA interview (Haraway, McLuhan, Warburg, Groys, Steyerl, Katrina Sluis).

## Use of fiction

Light but present: the "semi-fictional" performance of self; the implied narratives between images (couple → kiss; hand → superhero). Burbridge notes "metaphorical associations implied but never confirmed." The work sits at the parafictional edge of Fornieles' practice — less staged than *Cel*, but still a performed, framed version of the artist.

## Emergent behaviour

The defining feature: chains that begin feeling infinite ("you could go any direction… be totally free") reveal, after months, "a map of your limitations as well as a cultural landscape that relies on repetition, conventions and known patterns of behaviour." Loops back on themselves; meaning emerges from juxtaposition; "originality begins to collapse" and "this shared web of associations becomes apparent." Fornieles prizes such emergent systems over "predefined top-down image[s]."

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

40-minute film; Instagram chain (HeK feed, 8 Jun – 7 Jul 2021); five window-mounted inkjet print-maps at Carlos/Ishikawa (titles below); a booklet with Ben Burbridge's essay "On Ed Fornieles: Associations"; the CURA. interview with Giulia Colletti; a published protocol others can run. Fornieles says he will "return to this body of work when I need to" and wants to use association "as a tool to break down any subject matter." Images catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/associations.jsonl.

## What this project teaches the studio

Association is a portable studio method/tool — a way to "break down any subject matter, sort of parce it through myself, breaking it down into images and motifs." Teaches that exhaustive personal process exposes both the self's limits and shared cultural bias; that emergent, process-driven systems beat top-down authorship; and that publishing the protocol turns a private exercise into a collective instrument. (For Studio Clippy: association/juxtaposition is a sanctioned generative move; surface the bias in any retrieval, including its own; favour emergent over prescriptive structure; honour "attentiveness" as care.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

Fornieles' own stated worry that the work could be "merely… read as something about image culture, something purely conceptual" rather than personal — the success of the self-exposure is left open. The MDMA-assisted method was tried and abandoned. The participatory dimension (others running the protocol) was invited but its uptake is unrecorded in public sources. TKTK — confirm with Ed whether the protocol was widely played.

## Possible mutations

Involving other participants in expanded, collective association chains (Colletti raises this; Fornieles is open to it). Porting the association method onto specific subject matter as an analytic tool. Extending the chain into other media or into the crypto/Zien "Scarce Editions" distribution Fornieles was experimenting with in 2021. Returning to it as an "unblocking" exercise between projects.

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Treat association/juxtaposition as a legitimate generative tool, but always flag the bias baked into any image set or retrieval (including the assistant's own sources).
- Distinguish "attention" (passive) from "attentive" (caring participation); aim for the latter — "a duty of care" toward the image/idea.
- Prefer emergent, process-driven structure over imposing a top-down plan when Ed is exploring.
- Don't flatten the work to "it's about image culture" — keep the personal/subconscious stake visible.

**Key quotes — Ed Fornieles (fact, CURA. interview):**
- "I got sick early in the first lockdown… caught in [a] familiar loop of compulsive behaviour, Netflix, social media, pornography… platforms designed to push you into a soft zone state."
- Method: "placing a digital image and then connecting it to another through association, a formal, symbolic, or cultural connection."
- "After several months… what is being made is a map of your limitations as well as a cultural landscape that relies on repetition, conventions and known patterns of behaviour."
- "I have published the set of rules and protocol… it slowly begins to reveal unseen biases that are baked into our environments… the idea of originality begins to collapse."
- "Image searches… are insanely biased towards certain aesthetics and demographics… there has always been a visual protocol at play."
- "I wanted to perform myself in this ultra-honest mode, sort of hollow myself out through it… The whole thing ended up being like a broken map of my subconscious."
- "I experimented with MDMA to break my inhibitions but soon gave up on that."
- "Attention being potentially a passive focus while being attentive implies an almost caring participation… a duty of care."
- "I do not want to be in control, I do not want to be the director. I am interested in systems that are emergent."

**Search-engine finding (fact, Burbridge essay):** Fornieles "discovered that it is the Russian search engine Yandex, rather than Google or Bing, that retrieved and/or conjured the types of images he was looking for with maximum levels of efficiency."

**Critical framing (fact, Ben Burbridge essay):** Compares the maps to "a game of snake played on an old Nokia"; links the work to Aby Warburg's *Mnemosyne Atlas* (1929) and to Sebastian Schmieg's anecdote of Google misreading X-Factor's Chris Maloney as an aubergine; cites Hito Steyerl's "bubble vision," and Katrina Sluis: "a paradigm shift in which there is less value to be extracted from individual images than from relations between them." Closes: "the processes through which we become images, and images become us."

**Description of chains (fact, HeK):** "from the image of a hand to that of a family leading to a house and finally to the globe of the world… the globe to a dinosaur and the open palm of a hand to the superhero Tony Stark interpreted by Robert Downey Jr."

**Lineage (fact):** Linked by the artist to *Dorm Daze* (2011) and *Cel* (2019, 72-hour role-play with 10 participants), and to his concurrent psychoanalysis ("in analysis they love to associate"). Around 2021 he was also distributing "Scarce Editions" via the platform Zien.
