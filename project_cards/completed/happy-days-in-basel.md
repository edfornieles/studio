# Happy Days in Basel

## Short description

A Facebook sitcom edited by Ed Fornieles and Max Luz, made in connection with Theater Basel around its 2012/2013 season. The institution's season ran ~10 months (Sep 2012 – Jun 2013) while the Facebook sitcom ran only ~3 weeks; the Theater Basel season-preview publication invited readers to follow the theatre's programming and the Facebook fiction in parallel, producing "surprising collisions" between an institution's descriptive, information-focused voice and a fiction that mixed the real and the invented.

## Core question

What happens when a public theatre institution's official voice runs in parallel with an unofficial Facebook fiction — where do the two narrative registers collide?

## Social machine

A Facebook sitcom (cast of characters performing on the platform over ~3 weeks) deliberately set alongside the parallel "channel" of a real theatre's season communications.

## Fictional device

The Facebook sitcom — a short-run scripted social-media drama running beside (and bleeding into) Theater Basel's genuine season announcements. (Fact: Theater Basel season-preview framing.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Theater Basel and its institutional season / publicity apparatus; Facebook as a social stage; the relationship between a civic theatre and its public. (Fact.)

## Audience / participant role

Readers of the Theater Basel season preview were invited to follow both streams in parallel and watch them collide. (Fact.)

## Performer / subject role

A cast of Facebook-sitcom characters (edited by Fornieles & Max Luz). TKTK — interview Ed for who performed.

## Emotional engine

The friction/comedy of an official institutional voice rubbing against an unruly social-media fiction. (Inference.)

## Ethical danger

Blurring a public institution's real communications with fiction; potential confusion for the theatre's audience. (Inference.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: Facebook sitcom, Theater Basel, institutional voice, parallel narrative, mixed fiction/reality, German-language theatre context.

## Research method

Extends Fornieles' Facebook-performance method (cf. Dorm Daze) into a theatre-institution commission; collaboration with Max Luz on editing. (Inference + CV.)

## Use of real material

The real Theater Basel season, its season-preview publication, and the live Facebook platform. (Fact.)

## Use of fiction

The scripted sitcom characters and storyline running across ~3 weeks. (Fact.)

## Emergent behaviour

"Surprising collisions" between the theatre's descriptive content and the fiction. (Fact, framing.)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Documented in the Theater Basel Saisonvorschau 2012/2013 (designed by Ludovic Balland Typography Cabinet); listed on Fornieles' CV. (Fact.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That an institution's own communications can become one channel in a two-channel fiction — the studio can use a host institution's "official voice" as raw material to collide with. (Inference.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

TKTK — interview Ed (whether the 3-week sitcom landed against the 10-month season; reach/engagement).

## Possible mutations

Parallel-channel fictions attached to other institutions (museums, universities, festivals); longer-run sitcoms; other platforms. (Inference.)

## Venue / context

Theater Basel, Basel, Switzerland — 2012 (in connection with the 2012/2013 season). Season preview designed by Ludovic Balland.
