# Der Geist: Flesh Feast

## Short description

An exhibition (film, sculpture, objects, installation) chronicling Fornieles' attempt to follow a corporate-zen 'self-management diet', with his own body as the real site of intervention. The video 'Der Geist' (2016) centres on a cartoon fox and his success with the 'Bulletproof Diet', surrounded by figurines, printed wall-carpets, diet-packs and motivational messaging.

## Core question

How do wellness, productivity and self-optimization regimes colonize and reprogram the body and self?

## Social machine

A self-management/diet regime as a behavioural system, documented and satirized across media.

## Fictional device

A cartoon fox — Fornieles' recurring alter-ego — voiced by a man, narrating his "success" with the Bulletproof Diet across an eight-minute video ('Der Geist', 2016), 3D-printed figurines in glass vitrines, and motivational banners/wall-carpets. The fiction of the upbeat self-optimizing protagonist reframes the artist's real dietary experiment as a corporate-zen success story. (Fact: fox alter-ego, video, vitrines, banners — Schirn, Frieze.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Wellness/biohacking culture and productivity self-help — specifically the Bulletproof Diet (high-fat, low-carb regime invented by Dave Asprey; Bulletproof Coffee = espresso plus butter), and the broader "self-management diet" emerging from San Francisco programmer/tech communities that markets brain-function and high-performance optimization. (Fact: Asprey, Bulletproof Coffee staple, SF programmer framing — Frieze, Schirn, A*Desk.)

## Audience / participant role

A gallery visitor moving through a quasi-retail wellness display: vacuum-sealed starter-packs of espresso beans and fabric mounted on the walls (actually for sale at the show), fox figurine vitrines, and the narrated film. The visitor is positioned as a prospective consumer/convert to the regime. (Fact: starter-packs sold at the show — Frieze.)

## Performer / subject role

Fornieles' own body is the real site of intervention: he embodied the diet experimentally and documents the attempt. The fox is his fictional stand-in/performer. (Fact: "his attempt to adopt a self-management diet," body as site — Wikipedia, Schirn; inference: degree of literal adherence.)

## Emotional engine

The promise of an upgraded self set against the anxiety of inevitable failure. Fornieles: "I feel like I am continually failing, which I see as an unconscious by-product of these methods"; expectations are "nearly impossible to achieve; that failure is often a source of anxiety." Aspiration, self-discipline, and the dread of becoming "robotic" / losing humanity. (Fact: quotes — A*Desk 2018.)

## Ethical danger

Regimes of self-optimization that engineer body and mind toward goals can dehumanize ("If you become an ultimate efficient being… you begin to lose your humanity, or you become robotic") and embed patriarchal/corporate value systems while presenting as neutral self-care. The work also risks (per Frieze) merely "regurgitating" diet rhetoric without escaping the white-collar value system it depicts. (Fact: quote A*Desk; critique Frieze.)

## Aesthetic world

Glass vitrines of 3D-printed cartoon-fox figurines (fox on a psychoanalyst's couch; fox doing posture exercises on a chair; fox at a desk with eight Shiva-like arms holding corporate utensils); vacuum-sealed wall packages of espresso beans and fabric; a sculpture of two creatures made of vegetables and lemons; cartoonish infographics and printed banners/wall-carpets; the eight-minute video 'Der Geist'. Tags: self-optimization, wellness culture, the body, corporate zen, biohacking, Bulletproof Diet, diet, cartoon/animation, fox alter-ego, motivational messaging, 3D-printed figurines. (Fact — Frieze, Schirn.)

## Research method

Auto-experimental embodiment: Fornieles adopted/embodied the diet himself and documented the attempt, then translated it across film, sculpture, infographic and retail-display formats. He frames such methods more broadly as "revealing some of the mechanisms on how structures underline many of the environments we live in… by performing them differently." (Fact: embodying diets, "reveal" quote — A*Desk; inference: precise protocol.)

## Use of real material

Real wellness/diet commerce: actual Bulletproof Coffee ingredients (espresso beans for the espresso-and-butter staple), real starter-packs sold to visitors, references to Dave Asprey's product presentations, and Fornieles' own dieting experience. (Fact — Frieze, Schirn.)

## Use of fiction

The cartoon fox alter-ego and its narrated "success" story fictionalize and satirize the regime, turning a corporate-zen self-management diet into an animated motivational parable of identity "recoding." (Fact: fox, "recoding of one's own identity" — Schirn.)

## Emergent behaviour

The intended dynamic is a feedback loop where an immersive, seductive wellness pitch coexists with a reflective space that lets viewers recognize the regime's mechanics — though, per Frieze, the work risks the rhetoric overwhelming the critique. Personal by-product reported by the artist: continual failure and anxiety. (Inference + Fact A*Desk.)

## What this project teaches the studio

- Self-optimization/wellness regimes are systems that "take an approach to the individual and the body" — dieting as the pinnacle of self-management.
- The body can be the literal, real site of an artwork (auto-experiment), not just its subject.
- A cartoon alter-ego (the fox) lets the artist inhabit and satirize a regime from inside its own motivational language.
- Efficiency taken to its limit dehumanizes ("you become robotic"); failure is structurally built in and is the engine of the regime's anxiety.
- Critique that adopts a system's aesthetics risks being absorbed by it (the Frieze caution): hold the gap between immersion and reflection open.

## What failed or remained unresolved

Per the Frieze review (Bettridge, 2016), the show risked "regurgitating diet rhetoric and self-help slogans without bothering to posit anything beyond that very same white-collar value system" — i.e. the critique may not have escaped the corporate logic it depicted. Fornieles himself reports the diet experiment as ongoing failure. Whether viewers read the work as satire or as endorsement is unresolved. (Fact: Frieze critique, A*Desk failure quote.)

## Possible mutations

- A live retail/clinic version where visitors actually enrol in the regime and report back (feedback loop made literal).
- Other alter-ego species/avatars for other optimization cultures (sleep-hacking, nootropics, fasting).
- A gendered counter-version foregrounding the patriarchal coding Fornieles identifies in "brain-function" diets.
- Conceptual bridge to [[jupiter-ascending-sauna]]: the spa/sauna as the communal-ritual counterpart to the individual diet regime.

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Treat self-optimization/wellness systems as ideological machines acting on the body; name the corporate/patriarchal substrate.
- Use alter-egos and a system's own motivational language to reveal its mechanics from inside.
- Keep the satire/endorsement gap explicit; flag when a critique is being absorbed by its target (the Frieze risk).
- Foreground built-in failure and its anxiety as the emotional engine, not an accident.

## Venue / context

Arratia Beer, Berlin (Fornieles' first solo show in Berlin; 2016). The gallery later closed in 2019.

## Research dossier (web, 2026-06-10)

**Identity / dates (Fact).** *Der Geist: Flesh Feast*, 2016, Arratia Beer, Berlin — Fornieles' first solo exhibition in Berlin. Media: an eight-minute video 'Der Geist', sculpture, 3D-printed figurines in glass vitrines, vacuum-sealed wall packages, cartoonish infographics, printed banners/wall-carpets.

**The fox (Fact — Schirn, Frieze).** A cartoon fox is Fornieles' recurring alter-ego; in the video a man's voice narrates the fox's journey of self-optimization — memory exercises and workouts to sharpen the mind, leading to the "recoding of one's own identity." Figurines show the fox on a psychoanalyst's couch, doing posture exercises on a chair, and at a desk with eight Shiva-like arms holding corporate utensils.

**Bulletproof Diet (Fact — Frieze, Schirn).** The show references Dave Asprey, inventor of the high-fat/low-carb "Bulletproof Diet," who promotes a "high-performance life" via a biological "hack." Bulletproof Coffee (espresso + butter) is the staple; wall-mounted vacuum packs of espresso beans and fabric alluded to starter-packs Fornieles sold at the show.

**Key Fornieles quotes (Fact — A*Desk, 4 Jun 2018):**
- "all of these new-age lifestyles and diets are born out of a system taking an approach to the individual and the body."
- "If you become an ultimate efficient being at some point, you begin to lose your humanity, or you become robotic."
- "There are also hidden dangers and anxieties on these practices. You might have a set of expectations nearly impossible to achieve; that failure is often a source of anxiety."
- "I feel like I am continually failing, which I see as an unconscious by-product of these methods."
- On male "brain-function" diets from SF programmer communities: "these neuron-expanding ones do have embedded in themselves patriarchal concepts for sure."
- Method: art can "reveal and reprogram"; the gallery is "a social lab."

**Critical reception (Fact — Frieze, Thomas Bettridge, 2016).** The review judged the show to "regurgitate diet rhetoric and self-help slogans without bothering to posit anything beyond that very same white-collar value system," seeing something inherently corporate in the self-administered body.
