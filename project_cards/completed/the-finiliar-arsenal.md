# The Finiliar (Arsenal Contemporary)

## Short description

The gallery debut of the Finiliar concept: telematic LED works, inkjet prints, sculptures and animated video. Cute, Tamagotchi-inspired creatures were linked to real-time currency values (Canadian dollar, British pound, Ethereum) via synchronized LED screens. Included the HD video 'Tulip Fever' (2017, 9:21, ed. 5) and merchandise-style objects.

## Core question

How does global capital circulate through and animate everyday emotional/consumer objects?

## Social machine

Real-time currency-data-driven animated characters in a gallery installation.

## Fictional device

The Finiliar: per the press release, a "digital entity feeding off data sets produced by companies, currencies, and other large institutions, using a cute facial interface to elicit a sense of emotional attachment within the viewer." Named individual works personify specific currencies — *Dunop* (British Pound Sterling, £), *Radoll*, *Menuteer* — as Tamagotchi-esque characters. (source)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Foreign-exchange and crypto markets: three telematic LED screens synced to real-time short- and long-term value projections for the Canadian dollar, British pound, and Ethereum. (fact)

## Audience / participant role

Gallery visitor moved through a deliberately trade-show-like sequence: inkjet prints of the creatures → a front desk selling branded "office bric-a-brac" / swag → a black-walled, black-carpeted main room of glossy sculptures and LED monitors → a rear single-channel video that debriefs/advertises. The viewer is positioned to first find the creatures cute, then watch them perform the market, then be "implicated … in an empathetic relation to capital." (source)

## Performer / subject role

The animated Finiliars perform: "cavorting, sobbing, and even vomiting" to live currency feeds. The real subject is capital flow itself — the creature is "a manifestation of the market." (source: THE SEEN)

## Emotional engine

Cuteness → empathy → solemnity. The kawaii face produces instant aesthetic gratification, but seeing a currency's collapse rendered as a creature "huddled in a fetal position and crying" converts that into a reminder that economic fluctuation has real human emotional cost. (source)

## Ethical danger

The "trade show" staging knowingly courts the risk of being "shiny visual fodder shrouding a vapid idea" / aestheticising and selling the very market forces it critiques (swag, glossy sculptures, edition video). Mickleburgh notes the works forsake "the kind of data that makes meaningful market analysis possible" in favour of cute affect — a "daring move." (source)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: crypto, currency, data, capital, cuteness, installation, video, LED, merchandise.

## Research method

Developed during a residency at Arsenal Montréal (the Finiliars series originated there in 2016). Production as concept-cum-technology-cum-product: live FX/crypto data feeds wired to animated characters across LED screens, plus inkjet prints, sculptures, merchandise and a multi-soundtrack video — staged as an integrated brand/product launch rather than discrete objects. (source)

## Use of real material

Real-time market data for the Canadian dollar, British pound and Ethereum drives the LED works' animations live in the gallery. (fact)

## Use of fiction

Currencies are rebranded as named cartoon creatures (*Dunop*, *Radoll*, *Menuteer*) with their own characters; *Tulip Fever* (2017) is a single-channel video that imagines dramas and relationships between Finiliars as a corollary to each character's real-world market trajectory — and the rear video shifts between sincere "explanation" and "advertisement" depending on the soundtrack the viewer hears. (source)

## Emergent behaviour

Within an exhibition (not a live community) the "emergence" is interpretive: the show stages an encounter that flips the viewer from amusement to unease — the trade-show spectacle resolves into a question about real human cost. (inference from review)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Images catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/the-finiliar-arsenal.jsonl (project: the-finiliar-arsenal). Documented by Arsenal Contemporary, Artrabbit, Artnet, THE SEEN (Mickleburgh review, 27 Apr 2017). *Tulip Fever* later shown via the Malevich platform. This is the gallery seed that the 2022 [[finiliar]] NFT project grew from. (fact)

## What this project teaches the studio

That a concept can be launched as a total product/brand environment (prints + swag + sculpture + LED + video) and that the "trade-show" frame is itself a critique. That live data + cute face = empathy toward capital. The Arsenal currencies (CAD, GBP, ETH) are a fiat-heavy precursor to the later all-crypto ten-coin roster. (source + inference)

## What failed or remained unresolved

Open from public sources: the review flags the risk that the spectacle reads as "vapid hype"; whether the empathy effect survives the swag/commerce framing is left as a deliberate tension. Edition sizes/sales of the LED works and sculptures not publicly documented. (inference — confirm with Ed)

## Possible mutations

Extend beyond FX/crypto to institutional or civic data; the live-LED gallery form later mutated into the on-chain, owned, community-driven [[finiliar]] NFTs (2022). Could return as a museum-scale data-room. (inference)

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

Treat a show as a staged sequence of affect (seduce → implicate → debrief), and be candid that commerce/swag is part of the critique, not a betrayal of it. Keep work titles precise (*Tulip Fever*, *Dunop*, *Radoll*, *Menuteer*) and the currency roster (CAD/GBP/ETH) distinct from the later ten-coin NFT set. (inference)

## Research dossier (web, 2026-06-10)

**Exhibition (fact):** "The Finiliar," Ed Fornieles. Arsenal Contemporary New York's *inaugural* exhibition, 22 Feb – 23 Apr 2017 (opening 22 Feb, 6–8pm). 21 Cortlandt Alley, 2nd Floor, NY 10013. Developed during a residency at Arsenal Montréal; Finiliars series originated there 2016. Artist British, then based in Montreal.

**Works (fact):**
- *Tulip Fever* (2017), HD video, ed. of 5, 9m21s — single-channel; considers relations/dramas between Finiliars; ran with three different soundtracks (shifting between explanation and advertisement).
- Telematic LED works — *three* screens synced to real-time short- & long-term value projections for the Canadian dollar, British pound, and Ethereum. Named characters: *Dunop* (British Pound Sterling £), *Radoll*, *Menuteer*.
- Inkjet prints on dibond; glossy Finiliar sculptures; branded merchandise ("office bric-a-brac"); animated digital video.

**Press-release definition (quote):** "Digital entity feeding off data sets produced by companies, currencies, and other large institutions, using a cute facial interface to elicit a sense of emotional attachment within the viewer."

**Curatorial quotes:** "Fornieles creates stages for performative cultures stemming from the ebb and flow of information through various systems of value." / "Three looming LED screens … implicate the viewer in an empathetic relation to capital." [Arsenal]

**Review (THE SEEN, Elliot Mickleburgh, 27 Apr 2017):** describes creatures "cavorting, sobbing, and even vomiting" to feeds; "the Finiliar is more than a character; it is a manifestation of the market itself"; closes — "If a Finiliar weeps in the corner when Ethereum plummets, is there a human being of flesh and bone somewhere on this Earth doing the same thing?"

**Title note (inference):** *Tulip Fever* invokes 17th-c. Dutch "tulip mania," the canonical speculative bubble — pointing the work at financial irrationality.

**Inference vs fact:** dates, venue, works, currencies, residency = fact. Bubble/critique readings = inference grounded in the review and titles.
