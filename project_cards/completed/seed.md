# Seed

## Short description

A solo exhibition (Carlos/Ishikawa, 22 Sep – 28 Oct 2017) that gathers several strands of Fornieles' work on simulation, AI, data and emotional attachment, framing the gallery itself as an "imaginarium" and "memetic platform." It includes: the **Finiliar** (a trademarked cute digital creature whose emotional/physical states track live data such as stock prices, GDP, currencies and crypto, plus the animation *Tulip Fever*); **Mii Friends** (a friendship-as-a-service: six months of Fornieles' real Facebook account run by hired conversationalists across selectable "tones", later proposed for automation by AI, shown as foam-board avatars and inkjet "Conversation Reports"); **The Truth Table Experience** (a VR-and-bed installation cycling the viewer through ever-shifting bodies in sexual embrace to "level out" desire); **Babble Box** (a one-to-one audio booth designed to make trolling inefficient and force genuine political dialogue); and **Sim Vol.1: Existential Risk** (an A4 book/manual for running apocalypse role-play simulations). Probes how friendly, cute interfaces forge emotional bonds with abstract data systems, currencies, risks and one another.

## Core question

Can a meaningful relationship exist with an anonymous entity, service or A.I., and what happens when cute interfaces and simulations make us emotionally attached to — and able to internalise — abstract data systems, currencies, risks and other people? As the booklet asks: "how does friendship perform itself, what does it give us ... and what are the implications of such relationships?"

## Social machine

A cluster of "product"-shaped interfaces each translating an abstract system into affect: Finiliar (data feed → cute creature's mood/health), Mii Friends (emotional labour of paid conversationalists → automatable "friendship service"), Truth Table (VR → mathematically randomised, identity-levelling sexual experience), Babble (filter-bubble politics → enforced one-to-one audio dialogue). Sim Vol.1 is the meta-machine: a manual for building role-play "engines" run by a "PD" (play designer/director) for groups. The gallery is reframed as the machine's test-bed — a "memetic platform ... in which art is defined as proposition."

## Fictional device

Each work is staged as a near-future commercial product with promo copy, trademarks, and a slick onboarding voice ("Hello, I'm your Mii Friend"; "Truth is a new VR product engineered for your enjoyment"; the Finiliar "promotional video"). The fiction is the start-up / tech-trade-show register: empathy, friendship and desire packaged as scalable services. Sim Vol.1 fictionalises its premise as a self-help manual that "starts with an end" (apocalypse).

## Real-world system it attaches to

Live financial and institutional data (stock prices, GDP, GBP/CAD currencies, Ethereum and crypto, carbon/rainforest as proposed); Facebook (literally — Mii Friends ran on Fornieles' own account) and the data-harvesting model of social platforms; AI conversational agents / chatbots; VR and "experiential" sexual technology; recommendation algorithms and filter bubbles; political polarisation; and existential-risk / apocalypse discourse.

## Audience / participant role

The viewer is cast as user/customer/parent. With the Finiliar they become an emotionally implicated carer who is "rendered helpless," discovering that care "requires a complex choreography of persons, systems and models beyond their immediate control." With Mii Friends, gallery-era users actually chatted (anonymised, with consent) and the data fed the work. With Truth Table the viewer lies on a bed in VR and is cycled through bodies. With Babble they are pushed into a real one-to-one conversation. With Sim Vol.1 they are a potential facilitator who could run their own simulation.

## Performer / subject role

In Mii Friends the performers were hired "conversationalists" performing assigned tones (Conflict / Shoulder to cry on / Ego stroke / Inquisitive listener / Brainstorm / Flirtatious-Sexy) and filing weekly reports on "the emotional labour of responding to the needs of users" — explicitly framed as labour later to be displaced by AI. Fornieles is the author/operator (his own Facebook account was the platform) and, in Sim Vol.1, the "PD" who structures the simulation engine.

## Emotional engine

Cuteness and care as capture devices. The Finiliar weaponises "the exaggerated proportions of a newly born human child" to elicit parental bond and empathy toward illegible data; "a smile of a Finiliar connected to GDP may mark the creation of thousands of jobs." Mii Friends runs on loneliness and the desire for frictionless companionship; Truth Table on desire and its dissolution; Babble on bell hooks' "love as a disruptive force" against fear of the stranger. Throughout, affect is the residue the studio is after: work "untethered from the object ... registered in the trickle of thoughts and actions activated in the viewer."

## Ethical danger

Named inside the works. Mii Friends openly mirrors Facebook's data-harvesting and asks whether such friendships cause "extreme isolation," and stages the automation-away of human emotional labour. The Finiliar reveals a "reversal of expected roles": "in possessing a body, the Finiliar deprives the subject of their own, flattening them into another data set" — care for the cute thing displaces care for the people behind the data; the creatures embody a Darwinian market where "the older Finiliar ... begin to slowly die" as one absorbs market share. Truth Table flags the risk that identity-dissolution is not liberation but "a psychedelic hell." Babble worries it might "exacerbate and escalate tensions."

## Aesthetic world

Tags: simulation, artificial intelligence, data, kawaii/cuteness, emotional attachment, avatars, Mii, VR, existential risk, Finiliar, empathy tools, financialisation, filter bubbles, emotional labour, post-internet, product/trade-show aesthetics. Visually: glossy product/UX surfaces, LED screens of animated creatures reacting in real time, foam-board avatars, inkjet conversation reports, a VR headset on a bed, an aluminium audio booth — the gallery dressed as a soft-tech showroom.

## Research method

Long-duration live experiment plus practical manual. Mii Friends ran for six months on a real social-media account with hired staff and weekly analytic reports, iterating the "service" on real interaction data. Finiliar was built with Montreal-based engineers/designers who tested which facial features best generate empathy for abstract concepts. Sim Vol.1 codifies five years of role-play technique, adapting "acting theory, RPG tabletop games, emergency simulation and group therapy exercises" into reproducible "engines."

## Use of real material

Real live data feeds (stocks incl. Facebook's own stock, GDP, currencies, Ethereum); a real Facebook account and thousands of real (anonymised, consented) conversations and conversationalist reports; real emotional labour; a real bell hooks quotation as the ethical hinge of Babble. The Finiliar's moods are driven by genuine market movement, not scripted.

## Use of fiction

The product framing is the fiction: trademarks, promo voiceovers, the premise that conversationalists can be cleanly "replaced by an intelligent algorithm," the speculative leap to fully automated AI friends and to carbon/rainforest Finiliars. Sim Vol.1 fictionalises instruction as apocalypse-survival manual. Fiction lets each work behave as a proposition that "once realized can be deployed, held, felt, taken on or rejected."

## Emergent behaviour

By design. The booklet's thesis is "bleed" — work that crosses the gallery's "parameter line" and becomes active in viewers, "their groups and the structures they inhabit." Mii Friends evolved through emergent user interaction; Sim Vol.1's RPG engine "works through consensus of the group, who must collectively imagine and vocalize the world." The Finiliar ecology dramatises emergent market competition between creatures.

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

The Sim Vol.1: Existential Risk book (A4, 30×21 cm; also shown at Alyssa Davis Gallery, NY, 2017) is itself designed to seed further simulations. The Finiliar continued as an independent, expanding body of work (Arsenal Contemporary NY, 2017; later editions and an NFT/Web3 incarnation). Mii Friends Conversation Reports (inkjet, 233×246 cm) and Test Studies (18:17 video) and Tulip Fever (9:21 animation) are the documentary residue. Installation images catalogued in image_manifests/public_gallery_manifest.jsonl (project: seed).

## What this project teaches the studio

That an interface's *form* (cuteness, a friendly voice, a product frame) is an argument: it decides what you care about and how. That packaging a system as a service makes its politics — data extraction, the automation of emotional labour, the financialisation of feeling — legible and felt rather than merely stated. That the strongest works are "propositions" that bleed out of the gallery and live in the viewer's subsequent behaviour. And that the studio's role can be that of a "PD": building engines and rules within which others generate meaning.

## What failed or remained unresolved

The booklet leaves its own questions deliberately open ("Stay tuned to find out more"). Whether Mii Friends fostered closeness or "extreme isolation" is unresolved; whether Truth Table's identity-levelling frees or traumatises the subject is posed but not answered; whether Babble would de-escalate or inflame is uncertain. The page genuinely interleaves several distinct bodies of work, so the boundary of what was physically "in Seed" versus shown in sibling presentations (Finiliar at Arsenal; Sim Vol.1 at Alyssa Davis) is slightly porous. The promised full automation of Mii Friends by AI reads as proposition/fiction rather than a delivered system.

## Possible mutations

- A studio "Finiliar" attached to the studio's own metrics (energy use, attention, finances) as an affective dashboard.
- A consented "Mii Friend" assistant mode with explicit tone selection and a visible record of the emotional labour being performed/automated.
- A "Babble" protocol for the assistant: enforce slow, one-to-one, low-trolling dialogue when conversations turn adversarial.
- Running Sim Vol.1 engines as facilitation tooling for groups confronting existential/organisational risk.

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Be honest about the interface: when a "cute"/friendly frame is doing emotional work, name it; don't let the packaging hide data extraction or the displacement of human labour.
- Offer explicit, selectable conversational tones and a clear consent + stop mechanism ("stop conversation"), mirroring Mii Friends.
- Treat empathy as a tool with side effects: surface the systems behind the affect (the "complex choreography of persons, systems and models") rather than letting the user bond only with the cute surface.
- Prefer dialogue that maps "the underlying needs and desires driving" a position over scoring points (Babble / bell hooks).

## Research dossier (web, 2026-06-10)

**Dates / venue (fact).** Carlos/Ishikawa, London, 22 Sep – 28 Oct 2017; private view 21 Sep, 6–9pm (booklet + gallery page).

**Works checklist with media/dimensions (fact, gallery page, all 2017).** Sim Vol.1 (installation); Test Studies (18:17 min looped HD video); Sim Vol.1: Existential Risk (A4 book, 30×21 cm); Mii Friends (digital print on foam board, 200×220×133 cm); Mii Friends Conversation Reports (inkjet on paper, 233×246 cm); Finiliar (installation); Tulip Fever (9:21 min looped animation); The Truth Table Experience (VR set and bed, 190×90×50 cm); Test combination I & II (magnets on magnetic whiteboard, 120×180 cm each); Truth Table (installation); Babble Box (extruded aluminium, acrylic panels, audio equipment, 105×56×44 cm).

**Gallery-as-imaginarium (quote, booklet).** "At its best the gallery is an imaginarium, a place in which ideas can be fostered, modelled and tested." And: "The gallery at this point has changed function, operating as a memetic platform, a place of exchange in which art is defined as proposition."

**Mii Friends (fact + quote, booklet).** "Over a period of six months, Ed Fornieles' Facebook account was used by a team of hired conversationalists to test and develop a friendship service." Tones offered: Conflict / Shoulder to cry on / Ego stroke / Inquisitive listener / Brainstorm / Flirtatious-Sexy. Weekly conversationalist reports covered "the emotional labour of responding to the needs of users"; Stage 2 proposes replacing them with "an intelligent algorithm."

**Finiliar (fact + quote, booklet + Computerworld).** Defined (trademark): "digital entity feeding off data sets produced by companies, currencies, and other large institutions, using a cute facial interface to elicit emotional attachment." Built with Montreal-based engineers/designers using "the exaggerated proportions of a newly born human child." Fornieles (Computerworld, Thomas Macaulay, 12 Jul 2019): "This one is attached to the stocks of Facebook. And it's very happy at the moment"; possible future links "could be attached to carbon emissions ... the rainforest." Reacts to GBP, CAD, Ethereum/crypto (The Seen, Elliot Mickleburgh, 27 Apr 2017). Accompanying animation: *Tulip Fever* (references 17th-c. Dutch tulip-mania crash). The booklet flags a "reversal": the Finiliar "deprives the subject of their own [body], flattening them into another data set."

**Truth Table (quote, booklet).** "Truth is a new VR product engineered for your enjoyment ... offers users the chance to occupy an ever changing host of bodies locked in sexual embrace," producing "a leveling out, in which each type and form is given equal emphasis." Asks whether this frees the subject or is "a psychedelic hell."

**Babble (quote, booklet).** One-to-one audio designed so "the act of trolling or even angry discourse [is] incredibly inefficient." Hinged on a bell hooks quotation on love as "a disruptive force"; signed "All the best, Ed."

**Sim Vol.1 (quote, booklet).** "a practical manual to help you to harness the idea of simulation"; techniques adapted from "acting theory, RPG tabletop games, emergency simulation and group therapy exercises"; takes "the apocalypse and post-apocalypse as its starting point"; rules run by a "PD" are called "the engine."

**Press / reception.** The Seen (Mickleburgh) reads the Finiliar show as a mock tech-trade-show prioritising "instant aesthetic gratification" over data utility, generating empathy (a Finiliar weeping as Ethereum falls). Computerworld frames the Finiliar as an "empathy tool" demonstrating individuals' "miniscule" agency in large systems.
