# Animal House II: Revenge of the Nerdz

## Short description

The second iteration of Fornieles' immersive American-college-party performance, staged at the Royal College of Art, London, in 2011 (around the completion of his MA in Sculpture). It follows the first Animal House (Guest Projects, London Fields, 17 Jan 2011), in which 120 invited guests received character descriptions via Facebook RSVP and performed scripted actions drawn from college blogs, student Facebook profiles and frat-house films, inside sculptural sets. "Revenge of the Nerdz" reprises this machinery in the RCA context.

## Core question

Can an American frat-party — its archetypes, its escalation, its scripted abandon — be reconstructed as a performance, and what changes on a second staging inside an art school?

## Social machine

A staged college party as immersive performance: invited participants assigned characters and scripted actions, performing within built sets while camera crews document.

## Fictional device

The frat-party simulation — characters and actions sourced from college blogs, student Facebook profiles, and films (Animal House, Sorority Wars, Road Trip), with set-pieces like the "Shout" performance from the Animal House film. (Fact: Dazed, on the first iteration.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

American college / fraternity culture and its online documentation; frat-house cinema; the social ritual of the party; the RCA as institution. (Fact.)

## Audience / participant role

Invited guests become performers — given character descriptions in advance (via Facebook RSVP in iteration I) and instructed to perform scripted actions; the line between guest and actor collapses. (Fact for I; inference for II.)

## Performer / subject role

A mix of invited participants and hired actors (iteration I: 120 guests, 12 actors, 5 camera crews) performing college archetypes. (Fact for I.)

## Emotional engine

Disinhibition and escalation — the party "needs warming up," then builds to intensity; the thrill and discomfort of shedding inhibitions on cue (taped to walls, shaving foam, stripping). (Fact for I: Dazed.)

## Ethical danger

Consent and bodily exposure within a scripted party (taping, foam, stripping, groping in iteration I); the risk of real harm inside a fiction of abandon. (Fact for I; inference for II.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: frat party, American college culture, immersive performance, role-play, sculptural sets, RCA, camera crews, frat-house cinema, sequel.

## Research method

Mining college blogs, student Facebook profiles and frat films for characters and actions; casting; building sculptural party sets; documenting with multiple camera crews. (Fact for I.)

## Use of real material

A real party with real participants in built sets at a real institution (RCA); real social-media RSVP/casting apparatus. (Fact/inference.)

## Use of fiction

The assigned characters and scripted actions overlaid on a genuine social gathering. (Fact.)

## Emergent behaviour

Momentum builds past the script — iteration I "by eight-thirty it was really intense," with a collective "Shout" breakdown on the floor. (Fact for I.)

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

The first Animal House fed directly into the gallery work The Hangover Part II (2011, Carlos/Ishikawa), built from leftover materials. Photographic/video documentation; press (Dazed). Listed on Carlos/Ishikawa CV as a distinct 2011 work. (Fact.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That a social ritual can be iterated — restaged as a "sequel" in a new venue — and that the leftovers/documentation of a live performance become material for subsequent gallery works. (Inference.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

## Possible mutations

Further sequels; other genre-parties (prom, spring break, reunion); the party-to-gallery-residue pipeline (cf. The Hangover Part II). (Inference.)

Royal College of Art, London — 2011 (around MA Sculpture). Sequel to Animal House (Guest Projects, London Fields, 17 Jan 2011).
