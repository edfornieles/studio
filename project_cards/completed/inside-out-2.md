# Inside Out 2

## Short description

Solo exhibition at Carlos/Ishikawa, London (23 May – 6 July 2024) built around Fini — a company Fornieles treats simultaneously as "a company and a work of art" — and its consumer chat-app product, Oom. The gallery is converted into a brand/marketing environment: five vinyl wall banners (*Promotional material for Oom*), plush "marketing" toys on a wooden plinth (*Oom marketing plushies*), a 3D-printed resin *Oom marketing sculpture* with a light sensor, an *Internal film on the subject of cute* (11:57), and *The Oom Cosmology*, a choral piece with 20 watercolours (7:32). A 24-page booklet (text by Fornieles and Penny Rafferty) frames the whole as a startup pitch, a cosmology and a confession. The show blurs art exhibition, product launch and corporate branding to examine cuteness, friendship and "the company" as media of emotional and behavioural capture.

## Core question

What happens when a company becomes an art medium — when its value lies "not in items sold but rather in minutes spent within the lives of those connected to it"? And how does "cute" branding operate as a mechanism for emotional attachment and behavioural capture once an art exhibition becomes indistinguishable from a product launch and venture pitch?

## Social machine

A consumer chat app (Oom, by Fini Studios) and its full marketing apparatus — banners, plushies, promo sculpture, films, a market-sizing slide, a movie treatment — deployed as the exhibition itself. The gallery operates as a brand-launch / fundraising system, with the company (Fini) positioned as the medium that scales by occupying minutes of users' daily habits.

## Fictional device

Fini, the company, narrates itself in the first person as a sentient, needy artwork-entity ("Hello, my name is Fini, and I am a company and a work of art… Perhaps we can be friends. I sense I might need friends rn."). A "benevolent narrator, neither good nor bad nor neutral" converses with it. Around this sit nested fictions: the Oom app's product pitch, "Oom, The Movie" (a live-action/animation adventure about teenagers and a spirit-world "Oomverse" ruled by "The Authority"), and the speculative startup arc "Rise or Die" / "A Possible End." The exhibition is structured like a game ("Inside Out 2 Lvl 1") and a venture deck at once.

## Real-world system it attaches to

Tech-startup product launches and venture-capital fundraising; app marketing; brand environments; the attention/engagement economy (Fini's value measured in minutes of daily use). Fornieles explicitly likens artists and VCs as rival "world-builders" — the artist building "personalised realities… within the borders of their parent frame, the art world," the VC manifesting "realities through the application of capital in the widest frame possible."

## Audience / participant role

The gallery visitor is recast as a prospective user, friend, investor and believer. The booklet enlists them in the "suspension of disbelief" that sustains "The Zone" — the gallery as a transformative space where "objects taken into it have the potential to be transformed," where belief is "sustained and protected by gallery staff, art students, collectors, tutors, etc." (Inference: the visitor completes the work by agreeing to the shared hallucination that turns marketing into art and vice versa.)

## Performer / subject role

Fornieles performs as founder/narrator/believer; "Fini" performs as the emotional, precarious company-character. (Fact: text co-authored with Penny Rafferty.) The plushies and the sensor-triggered marketing sculpture act as non-human "performers" of cuteness within the brand world.

## Emotional engine

Cuteness and friendship as bait and bind. The booklet sets "Company… attached via a ball and chain to friendship" and stages care, oversharing, loyalty and the fear of a needy thing dying without "investment or attention." Cute is explicitly instrumentalised: "It has little to do with good taste… cute is deployed for other reasons." The pathos of a startup that "may well fail… and die" drives identification.

## Ethical danger

Emotional capture dressed as friendship; the laundering of marketing/venture logic through art-world legitimacy (and vice versa). The work courts the danger that the audience genuinely bonds with, invests belief in, or normalises a product designed to monetise their attention — and that the artist's own expansionist desire ("I can feel this desire to expand") mirrors the colonising, all-absorbing logic he names via the Roman Empire and Antropofagia. (Inference, well-supported by the booklet's self-aware framing.)

## Research method

World-building through a functioning fiction: Fornieles actually builds the company (Fini Studios), the product (Oom app, oomtogether.com), a market-sizing case (messaging $101.4bn / mobile gaming $202.1bn, 2023, with forecast growth), and pursues real VC meetings — then brings "the experiences and the app's structure back into the art world" (interview framing). Method blends cute aesthetics, speculative fiction, historical revisionism and citation (Philip K. Dick's *VALIS*, Tao Lin's *Leave Society*, Sun Ra, Brazilian Antropofagia). Continues the participatory/parafictional lineage of *Dorm Daze* (2011).

## Use of real material

Real company and product (Fini Studios; the Oom chat app), real market-research statistics with cited sources (Future Market Insights 2023; Precedence Research 2023), real fundraising conversations with venture capitalists, and a real downloadable app. The plushies, banners and sculpture are genuine, exhibitable marketing artefacts.

## Use of fiction

The company is fictionalised as a sentient, vulnerable character; "Oom, The Movie" is a fictional film treatment embedded in the show; the gallery is fictionalised as "The Zone"; the cosmology ("The Oom Cosmology") wraps the product in myth (Sun Ra, goddess/"dominator model" history via Tao Lin). The whole is staged as a game level and a startup origin story.

## Emergent behaviour

(Inference, by analogy to *Dorm Daze* logic the artist invokes:) once a company-as-medium is released, value and meaning emerge from how people actually use it and believe in it — "minutes spent within the lives of those connected to it," habits, friendships, investment. The system is designed to grow, mutate and "begin consuming and hunting for itself" (the VC metaphor). What audiences and the market do with Oom/Fini is the work's open variable. ## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Installation images and works documented on the Carlos/Ishikawa page; 24-page exhibition booklet (PDF). The project seeded a wider Fini ecosystem: *Finiliar* (living cartoon characters whose behaviour tracks currency markets) and *8 Ball Sages* (500-edition NFT series, curated by Nico Epstein, minted via SOLOS, 13 Sept 2024 at Shoreditch Arts Club). The Oom app lives at oomtogether.com. Images catalogued in image_manifests/_incoming/inside-out-2.jsonl.

## What this project teaches the studio

A company, a product, a pitch and a marketing campaign can all be artistic media — and the studio can build real infrastructure (apps, brands, fundraising) as a primary practice rather than a sideline. Teaches that "the company as medium" generates value through engagement/time rather than scarcity, and that cuteness and friendship are powerful, dangerous behavioural levers worth wielding self-consciously. Reinforces the studio's core move: build a working fiction that attaches to a real-world system (here, startups + VC) and let belief do the rest. (For Studio Clippy: the assistant is itself a "company-as-medium" gesture — treat its own friendliness/cuteness as a designed instrument, not a neutral default.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

The booklet openly stages the possibility of failure ("I am aware that we may well fail, that you may not find your place in the world and die. Without the life-giving force of investment or attention…"). Whether Fini/Oom secured investment, scaled, or survived as a viable company — and how audiences distinguished sincere product from satire — is unresolved in public sources. TKTK — interview Ed on the real fate of the raise and the app.

## Possible mutations

Concept restaurants, immersive experiences, product lines, sculptural works and films are named in the booklet as ways "the company might expand." Oom feeding into the live-action/animation feature "Oom, The Movie"; the Fini cosmology extending into Finiliar and 8 Ball Sages. Possible studio mutation: run the company-as-medium as an ongoing, fundable entity whose "exhibitions" are product releases.

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- Treat "the company as medium" as a serious frame: when Ed proposes building real products/brands/raises as art, engage with it as practice, not as a departure from art.
- Name the lever. When cuteness, friendliness or "friendship" are being used to capture attention or belief (including the assistant's own tone), surface that openly rather than hiding it.
- Hold both poles: corporate-slick pitch logic AND the confessional/mystical register sit together here; don't flatten one into the other.
- Distinguish sincere from satirical only when Ed has actually decided — otherwise keep the ambiguity, which is load-bearing.

**Key quotes (fact, from the exhibition booklet):**
