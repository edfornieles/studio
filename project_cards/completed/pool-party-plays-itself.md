# Pool Party Plays Itself

## Short description

A performance and video piece by Ed Fornieles produced in conjunction with MOCAtv (the Museum of Contemporary Art, Los Angeles' online channel) in 2013. Twenty-five actors enact a continuous, escalating role-play in an upscale Los Angeles bungalow, condensing archetypal Hollywood characters, scenarios and mythologies into a reality-TV format. The resulting film ("Pool Party") was distributed online via MOCAtv / Vimeo.

## Core question

What happens when the clichés of LA pop culture are staged as a self-running social system — can a "pool party" perform itself once you assemble enough archetypes in one place?

## Social machine

A Hollywood pool party run as a scripted-yet-improvised reality-TV shoot: 25 actors given archetypes and pop-culture cues, performing a single escalating sequence in one location.

## Fictional device

The "pool party" as a condensed reality-television format — a tapestry of Hollywood archetypes drawn from dating-contest shows, celebrity lifestyle programming, film-industry cautionary tales, and teen vampire dramas, woven into one continuous role-play. (Fact: MOCA description.)

## Real-world system it attaches to

Los Angeles entertainment culture; reality television; the museum's own move into online video (MOCAtv); the social ritual of the LA party. (Fact: MOCAtv production context.)

## Audience / participant role

Primarily a video work for an online audience; the live "audience" was the cast itself. Viewers watch a closed social world rather than entering it. (Inference.)

## Performer / subject role

25 actors in seamless role-play, each carrying a recognisable Hollywood archetype, improvising/escalating from pop-culture source material. (Fact: MOCA description.)

## Emotional engine

Recognition and unease — the pleasure of spotting the cliché plus the queasy sense of watching people dissolve into types. (Inference.)

## Ethical danger

The flattening of people into archetypes; the reality-TV logic of escalation for the camera. Lower stakes than the live galas since it is a staged shoot. (Inference.)

## Aesthetic world

Tags: LA bungalow, pool party, reality TV, Hollywood archetypes, teen drama, celebrity lifestyle, MOCAtv, online video.

## Research method

Casting + archetype-scripting drawn from TV/film genres (dating shows, lifestyle programming, teen vampire dramas), staged in a single real LA location. (Fact: MOCA description.)

## Use of real material

A real LA bungalow, real actors, the real MOCAtv platform and its audience. (Fact.)

## Use of fiction

The compressed reality-TV narrative and the archetypes layered onto the cast. (Fact.)

## Emergent behaviour

The "escalating sequence of events" suggests momentum building among the performers — TKTK confirm whether escalation was scripted or emergent. — interview Ed.

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

The film "Pool Party" (Short, 2013), distributed via MOCAtv (YouTube) and later Vimeo (https://vimeo.com/655572930); IMDb entry tt3332082 (rating ~7.4). Listed on Fornieles' gallery CV. (Fact.)

## What this project teaches the studio

That the archetype-casting method ports from live performance into a self-contained video format — a "social machine" can be filmed as well as enacted live. (Inference.)

## What failed or remained unresolved

TKTK — interview Ed (how the live shoot differed from the live-gala works; whether it felt too closed/cinematic).

## Possible mutations

Other genre-condensations (talk show, awards show, wedding) as self-running video role-plays; a participatory/online-streamed version. (Inference.)

## Venue / context

MOCAtv (Museum of Contemporary Art, Los Angeles — online channel), Los Angeles, 2013. Part of MOCAtv "ART X YOU" programming.
