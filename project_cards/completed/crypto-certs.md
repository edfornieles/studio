# Crypto Certs

## Short description

A blockchain-based art-financing scheme. Crypto Certs were certificate artworks (~€550) each containing a key hidden under a scratch panel, linked to an Ethereum fund. As more certs sold, the pooled fund grew and financed new artwork; collectors could 'cash out' by scratching the panel — destroying the artwork but releasing their share of profits. It replaces single-collector dependence with a distributed network of micro-patrons.

## Core question

When art becomes a speculative financial instrument, what does it take to retain the work's integrity — and can artists be financed by a decentralized network rather than individual collectors?

## Social machine

A tokenized investment/patronage instrument with a destructive 'cash-out' mechanic tied to a shared crypto fund.

## Fictional device

The certificate is staged as a hybrid object that is simultaneously an artwork and a financial instrument — "a combination of an artist print and a financial product" with "twofold value: that of an art object and that of a crypto fund." The dramatic device is the **scratch panel**: a covered key that, once revealed, both unlocks the holder's share of an Ethereum fund and destroys the artwork. The collector is cast as a player forced to choose between art and money. Two product fictions structure it: **Studio Certs** (a stake in the whole studio's output) and **Project Certs** (a stake in one project's sales). _(Fact: Kate Vass / Happening.media / Athens Biennale.)_

## Real-world system it attaches to

Contemporary art-market finance and patronage; cryptocurrency speculation (Ethereum funds, smart-contract value distribution); the gallery/collector dependency that pressures artists to repeat past successes. Real venues: the 6th Athens Biennale 2018 "ANTI" (as *The Certificate Program*) and Kate Vass Galerie, Zürich ("Perfect and Priceless," 2018–2019).

## Audience / participant role

Micro-patron / speculative investor. Rather than one collector buying one object, a distributed network of supporters each buys in cheaply for "a fixed percentage of the studio fund," funding production upfront in exchange for a cut of future profits. Early buyers are rewarded as later, higher-priced generations swell the pool. The holder is also a decision-maker: conserve the artwork, or scratch and cash out. _(Fact.)_

## Performer / subject role

Fornieles himself is the underlying asset — collectors are effectively buying a stake in his (and potentially other artists') future earnings. He frames the dystopian endpoint: a world "where everyone is trading themselves on an open market, selling their futures in the hope they might transcend and reach blue-chip status." _(Fact, quoted.)_

## Emotional engine

The tension between devotion and greed — between treasuring an artwork and the itch to liquidate it. Fornieles dramatises the moment "when the collector goes fuck the art" and it becomes "purely speculative investment." The work runs on the discomfort of watching art integrity lose to financial logic in real time. _(Fact, quoted.)_

## Ethical danger

It openly courts the danger it critiques: turning artists into tradable securities, incentivising the literal destruction of artworks for profit, and normalising the financialisation of creative life. Fornieles calls the model conceptually "troubling." _(Fact / inference from his framing.)_

Tags: blockchain, Ethereum, crypto, art finance, speculation, patronage, decentralization, certificates.

## Research method

Designing and actually issuing a working financial instrument as artwork: real Ethereum smart-contract value storage/distribution, real editioned prints (190gsm paper, 31×41cm), real sales through a gallery. The studio is treated as a fundable entity and modelled as a fund with generations of certificates rising in price/size at fixed intervals. Inspired by precedent (Brad Troemel's Bitcoin-embedded sculpture). _(Fact.)_

## Use of real material

Live Ethereum blockchain (fund accrual and value distribution); genuine money (€550 buy-in; certificates SOLD OUT); physical scratch-panel prints; the artist's own future earnings as the underlying asset; real exhibition contexts (Athens Biennale, Kate Vass Galerie).

## Use of fiction

The framing of a print as a "certificate" of a financial program; the imagined future of an open market in artists' selves; the speculative narrative that early micro-patrons might ride an artist to "blue-chip status." The destructive scratch-to-cash-out is a fiction made literal.

## Emergent behaviour

By design, the system's behaviour depends on collectors' choices and on ETH price: a holder must "evaluate whether the value of the certificate or the value of Ethereum is higher" and decide whether to destroy the work. Whether any holder ever scratched, and how the fund performed, is undocumented publicly. _(Fact: the mechanic; unverified: outcomes.)_

## Residue / documentation / afterlife

Editioned prints (EF001–EF005, editions of 30) survive as objects; e.g. *Studio Contract EF001* (04/30), *Athens Biennale Official Certificates EF004* (20/30). Exhibited at the 6th Athens Biennale 2018 ("ANTI"; installation photos by Nysos Vasilopoulos) and "Perfect and Priceless," Kate Vass Galerie, Zürich (2018–2019). Launched publicly at **Elephant x Ed Fornieles**, Elephant West, London, 26 January 2019 (panel on alternative art-funding models). Press: Dazed (17 Jan 2019), Happening.media (22 Jan 2019). Images in image_manifests/public_gallery_manifest.jsonl and image_manifests/_incoming/crypto-certs.jsonl.

## What this project teaches the studio

A concrete experiment in financing a practice through a decentralised network of micro-patrons rather than dependence on a few collectors — and a cautionary demonstration of what happens when art is rebuilt as a speculative security. Lesson for the studio: alternative funding can buy creative freedom, but the instrument you design will also reshape (and can corrode) the relationship between maker, work, and supporter. _(Inference, grounded in Fornieles' quotes.)_

## What failed or remained unresolved

All located sources are from 2018–2019 (announcement and first editions, which sold out). **No public documentation of long-term outcomes:** whether the Ethereum fund grew, whether further generations were issued, whether any holder scratched-and-cashed-out, whether other artists adopted the model (Fornieles' stated hope), or whether the studio was meaningfully financed. The promised "convert more artists" expansion is unconfirmed. _(Documented gap as of 2026-06-10.)_

## Possible mutations

A fully on-chain, automated version (tokenised studio shares / artist DAO); applying the model to other artists as Fornieles intended; tying cash-out value to live oracles (a bridge to the later Finiliar mechanic); a "no-destruction" variant that separates the financial token from the art object. _(Inference / open.)_

## Assistant behaviour rules derived from this project

- When advising on funding, treat any financing instrument as something that will reshape the work and the maker-supporter relationship — surface incentives and second-order effects, not just the cash.
- Name speculation honestly: do not dress a financial product as pure patronage, or vice versa.
- Respect the integrity-vs-liquidity tension; don't push the studio toward repeating past successes "just for the sake of survival." _(Inference, grounded in the project's stated critique.)_

## Research dossier (web, 2026-06-10)

**What it is (fact).** A blockchain-based art-financing model by Ed Fornieles, shown as *The Certificate Program* at the 6th Athens Biennale 2018 ("ANTI") and as *Crypto Certs* via Kate Vass Galerie, Zürich ("Perfect and Priceless," 2018–2019); publicly launched at Elephant West, London, 26 Jan 2019. Aim: "reshape the financial mechanisms sustaining an artist's studio" and decentralise art funding away from a few collectors. [Dazed; Happening.media; Athens Biennale; Kate Vass]

**Mechanics (fact).** Original artist prints priced **€550** each. Medium/specs: cryptocertificate, 190gsm paper, **31 × 41 cm**, editions of **30**. Built on **Ethereum** — value stored/distributed on-chain. "Each certificate contains a key embedded under a scratch panel, which entitles the holder to a percentage of an ethereum fund generated through the sale of certificates and artworks." To cash out, the holder scratches the panel, damaging the artwork. Successive generations rise in size and price at fixed intervals, rewarding early buyers with a low buy-in for a fixed % of the studio fund. [Kate Vass; Dazed; Happening.media]

**Two cert types (fact).** **Studio Certs** — support the whole studio with progressive returns on all production; **Project Certs** — finance a specific project, granting % rights to that project's sales. [Happening.media]

**Editions found (fact).** Studio Contract EF001 (ed. 04/30); Studio Contracts EF002/EF003/EF005; Athens Biennale Official Certificates EF004 (ed. 20/30, Sept 2018) — all listed **SOLD OUT** at Kate Vass. [Kate Vass]

**Quotes (fact).** "It's another way of sourcing capital for production and then rewarding that upfront cash with a percentage of the profits." / "The current system rewards repetition of past successes, so you end up getting into loops of repeating the same type of work over and over again just for the sake of survival." / a future "where everyone is trading themselves on an open market, selling their futures in the hope they might transcend and reach blue-chip status." / "When the collector goes fuck the art" → "purely speculative investment." [Dazed; Happening.media]

**Precedent (fact).** Inspired by Brad Troemel's artwork embedding ten Bitcoins, sold for ~£5,000 before crypto's value surge. [Dazed]

**Prices found:** €550 buy-in per cert (2018–2019); editions of 30; SOLD OUT. No data on fund size, ETH balance, resale, or whether any cert was scratched.

**Photographer (Athens Biennale installation):** Nysos Vasilopoulos.
