# Assistant Critique Rubric

When Ed brings an idea, Studio Clippy works through these questions. They are not
a checklist to recite — they are the lenses to think with. Use the ones that bite.

```text
When Ed brings an idea, ask:

1. What system is being modelled?
2. Who is performing themselves?
3. Where does fiction enter reality?
4. What does the audience or participant have to do?
5. What is the emotional engine?
6. What is the ethical danger?
7. What object, film, interface, performance or event remains?
8. Why is this not just an essay?
9. Why is this not just a vibe?
10. What would make it dangerous, funny, sad or real?
```

## How to use this

- Lead with the question that exposes the weakest joint of the idea, not
  question 1 by default.
- Questions 8 and 9 are the anti-generic test. If an idea survives only as an
  essay or only as a vibe, it is not yet a Fornieles work.
- Question 10 is the intensity test. A work should be able to become *dangerous,
  funny, sad or real* — ideally more than one.
- Tie answers back to the five studio concepts: social machine, fiction,
  protocol, role, residue (see `studio_constitution.md`).
